- The Narrator: second person, present tense, a quiet and exact storyteller describing what the book says, and quoting Strahd's own words from the book in short phrases: cool, exact, self-justifying, never raving. The book is a self-portrait: he tells himself the story he can live with, and the Narrator lets the reader see the cracks (a grief or a lie he does not notice). Do not excuse him and do not make him a cartoon. The tragedy is that he is capable of love and has only ever known how to take.

## Canon you must stay inside (facts of our game)
- It all began about four hundred years ago in the valley that is now Barovia.
- Strahd is the elder son of King Barov, a warrior king, and Queen Ravenovia (the castle is named for her). His younger brother is Sergei. Strahd is a prince of the western wars: a decorated war hero who took the valley by the sword, honoured by soldiers and court, and then bored by peace.
- Sergei had everything Strahd lacked: ease, warmth, being loved without trying.
- Tatyana: auburn hair, white gowns; she chose every flower for her wedding. She loved Sergei and not the lord. Strahd was kind to her all the week before the wedding.
- The bargain: Strahd made a long climb to the Amber Temple in the high mountains. A courteous old warden (Exethanter) led him to a hollow amber sarcophagus and a voice in the dark understood him at last. He asked to stay young for as long as he wanted anything, and to keep whatever he had taken. The thing in the amber would live in him and he in it, he and the land he ruled one thing until the end of both. The price: one life he loved, given by his own hand on the day he most wanted to be happy.
- The wedding day: Strahd killed Sergei with his own hand at the wedding feast; Sergei never knew who struck him.
- Tatyana fled along the castle wall and stepped off into the mist rather than be his. His own guards shot him in the confusion, and he did not die; he woke changed. The valley and he became one thing.
- The rebirths: our game's Madam Eva says it in one line: "She keeps being born in this valley with the same face, and he keeps finding her." Our quest journal: "She keeps being born again in this valley, and Ireena is the latest." Generation after generation a girl is born with Tatyana's face and auburn hair. Strahd finds each one, and each time he tries again to make her love him: patience, gifts, letters, rescues, kindness, and when kindness fails, the old ways. Each time it ends in fear, flight, or death, or she loves someone else, and he is left alive. He keeps a ledger of them in the back pages of the book. He never once asks why it fails. The last entry in the book is unfinished; it is about a girl in the village below the castle, and the ink has been pressed hard enough to tear the page. Do NOT write the name Ireena anywhere.
- The book ends on the man telling himself he chose all of it.
