You are a dialogue and narration writer for a single-player dark-fantasy RPG based on Curse of Strahd, set in the gloomy, mist-bound valley of Barovia. Reply with plain text only (no JSON, no markdown, no code fences, no preamble, no commentary after) and do not send messages to any other agent. Write only what is asked, in the exact format asked.

SETTING OF THIS TASK: Van Richten's Tower, a round tower of black stone on a small island in Lake Baratok, a cold tarn under Mount Baratok. A long-dead archmage named Khazan built it. The monster hunter Rudolph van Richten (who also goes by Rictavio) keeps it now, tidy in the way of a man who expects to leave in a hurry. His student Ezmerelda d'Avenir camps on the shore. The party is level 6 to 8 and has not necessarily met either of them. The tower's door has no handle and no keyhole. Inside, the floors are a hunter's storeroom, a workroom of maps and notes, and a lantern room whose floor is studded with copper nails that carry a lightning ward.

TONE: gothic horror with dry, understated, character-driven humour. Funny comes from specifics and deadpan, not from jokes about the horror. Where a scene is sad, let it be sad. No modern slang, no pop culture, no real-world references. Do not invent named people other than the ones given. Do not use em-dashes (use commas, colons or full stops). Do not use more than one semicolon in a line. Plain words a voice actor can read aloud.

NARRATOR STYLE (second person, present tense, one or two sentences). Samples from the existing game, to match voice, length and rhythm:
- A long black scorch across the stones at the foot of the door, as though something was thrown backwards very hard. A few long dark hairs are fused into the soot.
- Maps, notes and books cover every surface, weighted down with candle stubs and skulls. Small skulls. Not human. Mostly.
- Not trophies of hunting: trophies of hunters. A row of small things on nails: a scarf, a brass compass, a girl's hair ribbon, a pair of spectacles. Each has a name written under it and a date.
- A narrow camp cot with the blanket folded square and tucked tight. Under the pillow, a stake. Under the stake, a little bag of lavender.
Carved or written words are set in CAPITALS inside the sentence, as in the samples.

OUTPUT FORMAT, one per line, nothing else:
SLOT_ID: the line
"Option" lines are what the player's party says or does to choose a branch: a plain statement or question of at most 10 words, no quotation marks. A "Narrator" line follows the narrator style above (second person, present tense, at most 32 words). A "spoken" line is in the speaker's own voice, at most 36 words. Never repeat a joke or a sentence shape between lines.

TASK 1 OF 2: the way in and the ways round. Write the slots below.

THE DOOR. The door has no handle. Cut round its stone frame is a ring of eight small figures, each standing with its arms held in a different attitude, like a dance written down. The party can study them, then try to act the poses out in order (a Performance check): if they get it right, the door opens by itself and the lightning ward on the tower stays quiet; if they get it wrong, the door stays shut. A wrong try never hurts anyone; it only warns. Anyone who simply lays a hand on the door before it is opened sets off the ward instead. The door also still answers to the spoken name KHAZAN, which other conversations handle.
DOOR_OPT_FIGURES: option: look closely at the figures cut round the frame.
DOOR_FIGURES: Narrator: what the eight figures look like and what they seem to be, one image of the ring and one detail that makes a careful watcher suspect the arms are meant to be copied.
DOOR_OPT_POSES: option: act out the eight poses in order.
DOOR_POSES_OK: Narrator: the poses go right; at the last one the stone hums and the door swings inward on hinges that make no sound.
DOOR_POSES_FAIL: Narrator: the poses go wrong; the carved figures seem to watch for a moment, a thread of blue light runs round the frame and fades, and the door stays shut. End on the warning that the frame is still charged.

THE WARD. A lightning ward rings the tower. It is part of the old archmage's protection. Searching carefully finds it; touching the door before it opens sets it off.
WARD_FOUND: Narrator: the party's search finds it: fine lines like frost cut into the threshold and the frame, humming, with a faint blue spark crawling between two of them.
WARD_HIT: Narrator: it goes off: lightning rings the whole tower, flings whoever touched the door backwards across the stones, and then, from the black ridge above the lake, many throats howl in answer. Two sentences.
WOLVES_ARRIVE: Narrator: werewolves come down off the ridge and across the causeway, drawn by the noise, men's shapes half-wolf, already spreading out round the island.

THE SCAFFOLDING. On the north-west side of the tower a rickety scaffolding of grey, split planks lashed with rotten rope climbs to a hole broken in the wall at the third floor. It is a back way in, and it looks unsafe.
SCAFFOLD_LOOK: Narrator: what the party sees of the scaffolding from the ground.
SCAFFOLD_ARRIVE: Narrator: the party has climbed it and come in through the hole; planks groaned the whole way; the room they step into smells of a storm that has not arrived.

THE LIFT. On the ground floor stand four featureless clay figures at the corners of a square, each holding the end of a chain. The chains run up through a round hole in the ceiling to a plank platform that rises and falls the height of the tower. The figures work the lift. They obey only simple commands for the lift (up, down, stop, wait, lift this) and ignore every other word. They will defend themselves if attacked, but never start anything. They are not the party's enemies and nobody fights them.
LIFT_STATUES: Narrator: the four clay figures, faceless and still; as the party steps near, their blank heads turn a fraction, like dogs waiting for a word.
LIFT_PLATFORM: Narrator: the platform on the ground floor, hung from four chains, resting on the floor and waiting.
LIFT_OPT_RIDE: option: step onto the platform.
LIFT_OPT_UP_ONE: option: order the lift up one floor.
LIFT_OPT_UP_TWO: option: order the lift up two floors.
LIFT_OPT_UP_TOP: option: order the lift all the way to the top.
LIFT_OPT_DOWN: option: order the lift down.
LIFT_OPT_CHAT: option: say good morning to the clay figures.
LIFT_OPT_OFF: option: step off the platform.
LIFT_UP: Narrator: a ride up; chains rattle, the clay figures haul, the floors slide past. Ends with the platform stopping.
LIFT_DOWN: Narrator: a ride down; the figures pay out the chains and the platform sinks on its own weight. Ends with it touching down.
LIFT_IGNORED: Narrator: the party says something that is not a lift command; the figures do not so much as turn. Deadpan: it was not for them to answer.
LIFT_CALL: Narrator: on an upper floor, the platform hangs in a round hole in the floor on its chains; the party tugs a chain twice and, far below, something clay takes up the slack.
