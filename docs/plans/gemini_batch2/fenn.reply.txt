== meet
Narrator: A neat man in a good coat gone shabby at the cuffs stands by the signpost, pencil in hand and satchel at his hip.
Narrator: He squints at the rotten wood, makes a small mark in a thick ledger, and looks up as you approach.
Fennimore [smile]: Good day. Make a note, if you keep notes: the crossroads signpost is one star. Legible, if you already know where you are.
Fennimore: Ambrose Fennimore, at your service. Eleven years in this valley, and nine hundred pages into its only reliable guidebook.
Fennimore: It is a work of great patience. Every inn, every bridge, every moderately polite ghost, all graded on a five-star scale.
Fennimore: Tell me, have you by any chance come up from the Vistani camp at Tser Pool?

== book
Fennimore: Look here. I keep the proofs in order of the road, so a traveller may read as he walks.
Fennimore: "The Crossroads Signpost: one star. Points faithfully toward two places you should not go."
Fennimore: "The Crossroads Gallows: one star. Serviceable timber, poor view, no seating."
Fennimore: "The Blue Water Inn, Vallaki: two stars. Wolves." And beneath that, "Tser Pool Camp: three stars. The plum stew is excellent."
Fennimore [weary]: Only I have underlined "plum" twice in pencil. My tongue says onion, hare, woodsmoke, and something considerably older than a fruit.

== ask
Fennimore: A guidebook that guesses at its soups is no better than a rumor, and this valley has rumors enough.
Fennimore: I would go back and ask Old Dorina myself, but she looks at my notebook the way a butcher looks at a stray dog.
Fennimore: If your road takes you past her fire, find out what is truly at the bottom of that iron pot.
Fennimore: I will pay ten gold pieces for the verified recipe. An unconfirmed fact is a blemish on the page.

== accept_note
Fennimore [smile]: Most obliged. You will find me here by the signpost, or on the road, or wherever the road chooses to leave me.

== dorina_ask
Old Dorina: What is in it? Plums, child. Good dark plums, four big onions, and a fat hare Radu caught by the water.
Old Dorina: A pinch of salt, a heel of black bread to thicken the liquor, and whatever herbs were not drowned by the rain.
Old Dorina [weary]: That is all that went in today. What was already waiting for it in the iron is another matter.

== dorina_taste
Narrator: You lift a spoonful from the dark broth. It is sweet with plum at first, then smoky, rich with onion and game.
Narrator: Underneath all three sits a deeper, heavier taste, dark as cellar stone and older than anything grown this year.
Narrator: Old Dorina watches your throat as you swallow, her hands resting flat on the handle of her ladle.

== dorina_truth
Old Dorina: Sixty years that pot has hung over my fire, and for sixty years no one has seen the iron at the bottom.
Old Dorina: Every night we eat it down to the lastthree fingers, and every morning I build the new stew on top of the old.
Old Dorina: The stock at the very bottom is older than Stanimir. It remembers winters his mother forgot.
Old Dorina [sad]: The night my husband walked out toward the road and did not come back, my silver wedding spoon slipped from my hand into the boil.
Old Dorina [sad]: I never fished it out. You do not empty a pot that keeps the camp alive just to go looking for silver.
Old Dorina [smile]: So it stays down there, seasoning the broth. Nobody who eats from my ladle leaves with a thin belly.

== dorina_shy
Old Dorina [angry]: Enough questions! What are you, clerks for the devil up the hill, counting every onion in an old woman's pot?
Old Dorina: Sit down, finish your bowl while it has heat in it, and keep your noses out of my iron.

== report
Fennimore [smile]: Sixty years! Never emptied once, and a silver wedding spoon resting at the bottom like a river stone.
Fennimore: Oh, that is first-rate. That is the sort of detail a man stays eleven years in the fog to record.
Fennimore: Let me rub out that shameful little question mark and write it properly while the lead is sharp.
Fennimore: "Tser Pool: three stars. The stew is plum, onion, hare and sixty years. Ask for the bottom."
Fennimore [smile]: Read that aloud and tell me it does not make you hungry and slightly reflective at the same time.
Fennimore: Here are your ten gold pieces, well earned and duly noted under research expenses.
Fennimore: I am off toward Vallaki next. If you find any of my facts wanting along the road, do tell me before I go to press.

== after
Fennimore [weary]: Ah, it is you. I am currently grading the afternoon weather: one star. Damp, grey, and entirely derivative of yesterday.
Fennimore: You have not heard from anyone up the road whether it intends to improve before winter, have you?
