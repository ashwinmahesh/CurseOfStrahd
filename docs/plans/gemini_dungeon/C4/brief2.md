You are drafting text for "Curse of Strahd", a single-player CRPG built on the D&D 5e Curse of Strahd campaign. Reply with ONLY the slot lines in the format at the end (no preamble, no commentary, no code fence).

## Who the Narrator is
An old gothic storyteller who has watched a great many adventurers walk into Barovia and very few walk out. Not the party's enemy and not its friend. Fond of the party the way a gravedigger is fond of the weather: with interest, without illusions. Never gloats over a failure, never cheers a success. Notices.

## Rules
1. Second person, present tense. "You step inside." Never "the party steps inside", never past tense. Never use character names (the lord of the castle is "the count" or "the master of the house", never his name).
2. One or two sentences per line. The second sentence is usually the turn: the irony, the dread, the joke.
3. Ominous, dryly funny, never cruel. Laugh at the house, the dark, the situation. Never at the player.
4. No facts the party hasn't earned. Describe only what the characters can perceive.
5. No modern slang, no winks at the player, no game terms (no "HP", "roll", "level", "action", "spell slot", "save", "check", "DC", "damage").
6. Concrete senses over adjectives: a smell, a sound, a temperature.
7. Plain punctuation: commas, colons, full stops. No em-dashes, no semicolons, no ellipses.
8. Each Narrator line is at most 35 words. Most are 18 to 28. Option labels (marked OPTION) are at most 10 words, written as what the party does ("Wait for the fire to burn low.").
9. Respect the dead: be wry about a corpse's circumstances, never about the corpse.

## The place
Castle Ravenloft is the home of the vampire lord of Barovia. These slots are about a hidden way far below the castle's dungeon, which we are adding, in our own words. All of it is ours, not copied from any book, so invent small concrete details freely, but keep to the facts below.

- THE LOW DOOR: in the south wall of the dungeon hall, behind a cold iron brazier, stands a low iron door that is easy to miss. Behind it a narrow stair winds down into the dark.
- THE WESTERN STAIR: a long stair of worn steps going down, turning at a broad landing. In the landing floor a warding glyph is cut into the stone: a circle of pale lines. Whoever steps on it raises an image of the count, tall, pale, in black with a red lining, who comes at the intruders. The image is made of wax: the first blow that lands melts it into a puddle of wax and a smell of candle-ends. Past the landing the stair goes on down to the brazier room.
- THE BRAZIER ROOM: a square vaulted chamber with a high ceiling. In its middle stands a broad stone brazier burning with a tall white flame that gives no heat at all. Around the brazier's rim are seven cup-shaped hollows, each holding a smooth coloured stone: violet, orange, red, green, indigo, blue and yellow. Above the brazier an hourglass hangs in the air by nothing, all its sand in the top bulb, and a verse is cut round its base. Throwing one of the stones into the flame turns the flame that stone's colour and makes the sand begin to fall, and then anyone who steps into the fire is carried away in a blink to another place far off. In two deep alcoves stand two iron knights, huge, visors down. They are really golems and they move only if someone attacks the brazier, the hourglass or one of them.
- WHERE EACH STONE LEADS (the verse must hint, never name outright):
  violet: the white guard tower high in the snow of the mountain pass.
  orange: the open roofs and peak of this same castle, under the sky.
  red: a tall house of lore and learning, the tower of an old monster hunter.
  green: where the coffins are kept, the castle's catacombs.
  indigo: the rooms where the count's brides sleep.
  blue: the ancient temple of amber buried in the mountain, where old magic was born.
  yellow: the count's own tomb.
- THE CENTRE STAIR: a second flight down from the south side of the brazier room, ending in a low black passage with fog creeping up it.
- THE TUNNEL: a long low tunnel cut through the black rock the castle stands on. Fog drifts along the floor, the ceiling barely clears a tall person's head, the floor is wet and slick, and the stonework is newer than the castle above it. Somewhere along it a hidden trapdoor of polished stone tips whoever steps on it into a chute. The tunnel ends at a low arch into the castle's catacombs.
- THE CHUTE: a steep chute of black polished marble, slick as ice, with no handholds. It drops the unwary into a prison cell under the dungeon hall, and it cannot be climbed back up without magic.
