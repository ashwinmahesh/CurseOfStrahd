It needs eggs (Goodwife Marta in the market), dark honey (Old Paraschiva, the cook at the Vistani camp outside the walls) and saffron (Vadoma, a Vistana trader who sells from a painted wagon at the same camp). Vadoma is wry and sharp: she sells dear and never gives anything away.
Earlier in the story the party fought the Vistani of the Tser Pool camp, a day's walk away. The Vistani everywhere now know it. Vadoma already says, to people who did that: "No. Not today, not for your gold. Every wagon in the valley knows what you did. Walk on."
When the cake is finished, someone tastes it before the household and gives the verdict. Until now that was always the Baron (Vargas Vallakovich, a round, frightened, over-cheerful burgomaster), but the Baron is no longer in power in two of the story's endings:
(a) Lady Wachter (Fiona Wachter, a widow of impeccable composure, silk over steel, "my friends", pours excellent tea, never raises her voice, treats everything like a dinner party) now rules Vallaki from the Baron's chair in the mansion. There are no more festivals; the town keeps a curfew.
(b) The Baron has been deposed by a council of householders (Father Lucian and Urwin Martikov speak for it), and still lives in the mansion, in his shirtsleeves with the festival ledger open; the council lets him keep the sash. Without a festival to run he is sad and still kind of cheerful; "Lucian insisted. He's kind, Lucian. I always thought it was gloom."
The cake comes out one of two ways: GOOD (eight crisp golden points, saffron-bright, a little lopsided) or FLAT (the points have melted into a wide, bubbling golden puddle).
