You are drafting dialogue lines for a Curse of Strahd video game (D&D 5e, the valley of Barovia). Reply in plain text only, no JSON, and do not send messages to anyone else.

THE CHARACTER
Khazan is a lich in a side quest of our own, "The Name Over the Door". He was archmage to the valley's old kings before Strahd's family came, built the tower on Lake Baratok (now Van Richten's Tower), and went "downstairs" into an undercroft under the tower's hearth. He became a lich the cheap way: his phylactery is his own name, cut over the tower door, and everyone who says it to get in keeps him in the world. He keeps a great book of every visitor's name. He is courteous, curious, very old, bookish and dryly polite; he finds rudeness worse than murder. He is not Strahd's servant ("I was here first").

THE PROBLEM
Players find him not evil enough, with no reason to fight him: right now he is a polite old scholar who says he'll "write you down". He must stay polite and soft-spoken, but the player has to come away certain he is a monster who eats people and is coming for people they care about.

WHAT HIS EVIL IS (our design; keep to it)
- Most names he only reads: being said and remembered keeps him alive.
- When he's hungry he writes a name a second time, in "the good ink" (red). The owner of that name then comes downstairs one night, sleepwalking, and never goes back up. He eats what they were; what's left stays down here as one of his "Visitors" (wraiths that fight beside him) or as a name crossed out in red in the hall of names below his study ("Read. Kept.").
- In his book the newest names are EZMERELDA D'AVENIR (the monster hunter who lives in the tower above), RUDOLPH VAN RICHTEN written twice (the second time in red, half finished: he keeps leaving the valley before it's done), and the party's own names, ink still wet. So he is coming for Ezmerelda, Van Richten and the party.
- He'd let the party go, for a price: bring visitors to his door, people who will say his name (three names for each of theirs). A vile, casual bargain. The player can only refuse or walk away; don't write an acceptance.

WHAT TO WRITE (each line one sentence or two, at most about 30 words; Khazan never shouts)
1. Node "who", one extra Khazan line after he says who he is: a hint of his hunger, said lightly.
2. Node "ink" (the player asks "What happens to the names you write in red?"): 3 or 4 Khazan lines explaining the good ink, the sleepwalking guests, the Visitors, and that Van Richten is half written and Ezmerelda is next. Polite, matter-of-fact, a little proud.
3. One short party interject after the ink reveal, from a blunt-spoken hero (tag:blunt), and one from a kind-hearted hero (tag:kind). One sentence each.
4. Node "bargain" (the player says "Strike their names out."): 2 or 3 Khazan lines refusing and offering the bargain: send him visitors, three names for each of theirs, and he'll cross the party out with a ruler, not red ink.
5. Two Khazan lines for when the fight starts (node "fight"), where he calls his Visitors down by name: two ordinary Barovian first names, people who once visited the tower (a hunter, a girl who carried the post, a priest; your pick), so the player understands the wraiths were people.
6. One Narrator line (second person, plain, a little dry) for the book of names: Van Richten's second entry, in red, stops halfway through the last word.
7. One replacement for his greeting's last line when the party did not say his name at the door. Current line: "I don't know your names, I'm afraid. You never said mine at my door, and the door's bargain is a name for a name. It's a little rude, coming in without it. Still, you're here now. I'll write you down anyway." Make the last sentence quietly threatening.

STYLE
- Plain, modern, slightly British English; short sentences; no flowery fantasy diction; no em dashes (use colons or full stops); dry wit is welcome.
- Stage directions in parentheses are allowed at the start of a line, e.g. "(He's delighted.)".
- Don't invent other canon: no new places, no named kings, nothing about Strahd beyond "he has never said my name".
- Label each line with its item number, e.g. "2a Khazan: ...".
