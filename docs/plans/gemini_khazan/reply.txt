1 Khazan: Reading suffices for the centuries, mostly. Though every few years one does fancy a proper meal, and a book is no good at all if you never finish a chapter.

2a Khazan: (He taps a stoppered red bottle.) The black ink is only for taking notes. When I get hungry, I write a name a second time, in the good ink.

2b Khazan: Once a name is dry in red, its owner walks down my stairs in their sleep. They never wake up, and they never go back.

2c Khazan: I eat what they were, which is very filling. What is left stays down here to keep me company, and I cross them out downstairs.

2d Khazan: Old Rudolph is half written already: he keeps leaving the valley before the ink dries. So I think I shall have young Ezmerelda upstairs for supper instead, and then you.

3a Party (tag:blunt): He's not a librarian, he's a spider with a ledger, and he's got our friends on his menu.

3b Party (tag:kind): Those things in the dark were just travellers who knocked on your door, and you ate their lives.

4a Khazan: Oh, I never cross out a name before I've eaten it. That would be a terrible waste of ink, and Ezmerelda is already right upstairs.

4b Khazan: Still, we could be sensible. Send other travellers to my door to say my name: three new visitors for each of yours.

4c Khazan: Do that, and I'll rule a neat black line through your names and let you walk out. Refuse, and I'll fetch the red bottle now.

5a Khazan: Marta, dear, you used to bring the post: do come and show our guests where the stairs end.

5b Khazan: And Sorin. You were a hunter before you came down in your sleep: see that they don't reach the door.

6 Narrator: Below Ezmerelda's name, "Rudolph van Richten" is written a second time in wet crimson. The nib stopped halfway through the "n", waiting for him to come home.

7 Khazan: I don't know your names, I'm afraid. You never said mine at my door, and the door's bargain is a name for a name. It's a little rude, coming in without it. Still, you're here now. I'll skip the black ink and write you down in red.
