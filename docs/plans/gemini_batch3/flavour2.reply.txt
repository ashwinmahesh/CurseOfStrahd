== gheorghe_pictures
Gheorghe: The Baron says the mud must be raked smooth by eight, so I draw something in it first, a different picture every morning.
Gheorghe [wry]: Today's was a three-masted boat, though I have never seen the sea, and then I pulled the tines through and made it street again.
Gheorghe [afraid]: Do not mention the boat to the watch, if it's all the same to you; the Baron has opinions about where people's minds go.

== kolya_sun
Old Kolya [weary]: Fifty years I have hauled the willow up from the river, and fifty years I have watched them bend it into a ball.
Old Kolya: The very first year, I crept into the square three nights early with a tallow stub and set the whole thing blazing.
Old Kolya: Old Baron Varga had the stocks full for a fortnight, but he never found out it was the carter who brought the wood.
Old Kolya [sad]: Look at these knuckles now; I have built two thousand suns since then, and not one of them kept my hands warm.

== costel_velvet
Master Costel [smile]: Yellow linen, yellow wool, yellow ribbon by the bolt; if it looks like butter or brass, the town has bought me clean out of it.
Master Costel [sly]: Of course, a sensible merchant keeps one roll of good, heavy black velvet wrapped in oilcloth behind the cellar stairs.
Master Costel: I am holding that back for afterwards, and no, I will not say after what, though I have the yardstick ready.

== herald_jokes
Festival Herald: I write the proclamations myself, you know, in the dry room above the cistern before the guards wake up.
Festival Herald [sly]: The last six have been jokes, including the one declaring rainwater a form of applause, and the Baron signed every page.
Festival Herald [weary]: Excuse my cough; shouting about mandatory joy in the damp takes the lining right off a man's throat.

== warden_key
Lockup Warden [weary]: The iron key hangs on the crooked nail just outside the bars, where any tall man with a long coat-sleeve can fish it down.
Lockup Warden: On festival nights I take my supper at the far end of the hall with my boots off and my back turned.
Lockup Warden: In twenty years I have never lost a prisoner who did not badly need losing, and the morning shift never counts past three.

== gunther_list
Gunther Arasek: Honest timber, iron nails, and salt pork in barrels, with the grey wolves on the Svalich road counted into the freight price.
Gunther Arasek [sad]: I keep a ledger in my desk with the name of every driver taken off the box, and their widows get the winter share on the first of the month.
Gunther Arasek: The book stays locked in the drawer; it is an account between me and the road, and not for customers to read.

== vadoma_cards
Vadoma: I used to lay the painted cards out on the velvet, back when they still had the manners to speak when spoken to.
Vadoma [sad]: Six months ago they went quiet under my thumbs, every king and knave as blank as a river stone, though I have told no one in the tents.
Vadoma [weary]: Now I sell brass lockets and bottled teeth from the wagon tail, because a dead man's trinket cannot lie to me.

== piccolo_perch
Narrator: On his carved perch behind the counter, Piccolo the monkey regards you over his stitched red waistcoat with the cold, unblinking appraisal of a moneylender.
Narrator: He extends one small, grey palm and taps a fingernail against the wood until you understand that looking at the toys is not free.
Narrator: A gold piece leaves your fingers and vanishes into his waistcoat pocket.
Narrator: You watched him the entire time, and you still could not say how the coin got there.

== piccolo_catch_ok
Narrator: You catch the tip of Piccolo's tail just as he slips along the wainscoting toward the high shelves with your gold.
Narrator: He stops, turns slowly, and presses the warm coin back into your palm with an expression of elaborate, weary contempt.
Narrator: Smoothing his tutu with both hands, he climbs back to his perch and refuses to look at you for the rest of your visit.

== piccolo_catch_miss
Narrator: The monkey is much faster than your hand; he makes three silent bounds up the clockwork shelves and drops your coin into a stuffed jester's hat near the ceiling.
Narrator: Peering down over the brim with his chin in his hands, he looks deeply, professionally pleased with himself.
