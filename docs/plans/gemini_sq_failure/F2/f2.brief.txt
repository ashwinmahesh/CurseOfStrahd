You are drafting short text for a dark-fantasy RPG based on Curse of Strahd (D&D 5e, the valley of Barovia, ruled by the vampire Strahd). Reply in plain text only, no JSON, no markdown headings, and do not send messages to anyone else. Finish after one reply.

TASK A: ten journal entries. The game's quest journal is written in the party's own voice ("we"), past tense, plain and specific, one or two sentences, no more than about 40 words each. When a hero of the party is lost for good (they gave their life on the altar beneath Death House, or a card from the Deck of Many Things took them), their personal quest closes as a failure. Write the closing journal entry for each. Name the hero and the unfinished errand concretely; say they will not finish it. Do not say how they were lost (it differs), do not blame anyone, no lecture, one quiet specific image is welcome. Never invent new people or places beyond what is given.

The ten quests (hero, what they were trying to do):
1. A Seat at the Table: Ratatouille, a cook who learned magic so people would sit and eat with him; he wanted to apprentice to the Wizard of Wines and one day cook a proper supper for his friends and call himself a wizard.
2. Aurel's Last Chapter: Silvain Aster, a scholar, was looking for his colleague Aurel Mirescu, who went into the mists after a book written in a vampire's own hand.
3. Carry the Dawn: Liriel Dawnsong, a cleric of the Morninglord, was told in a vision to carry the dawn into this valley; she meant to sing real dawn into Castle Ravenloft's chapel.
4. Cold Prayers: Hedda Ironvow, a cleric, followed a dream of a dying sun and a silver raven; she meant to carry the raven's sun into the devil's chapel.
5. Grandda's Trail: Thistle, raised in the deep woods by the trapper Fen Burley, was following Fen's old trail blazes; Fen followed a great wolf into the fog years ago.
6. Count Off: Ilse Varga, a mercenary, was counting off her lost company, Kestrel Company, name by name, and meant to call the roll to the end in the castle.
7. The Fine Print: Kip Smudgewick, a halfling who signed a devil's contract (the devil is Mister Quillon) to save his family's apricot orchard; his way out needed a signature from the lord of this valley.
8. A Knight, More or Less: Godrick Pendlebrook was knighted on a deathbed with a soup ladle and carries his old master Sir Aldric's sword to be knighted properly.
9. The Last Lesson: Wren Featherfoot carried a sealed letter from his dead master Sorrel to Sorrel's old student Rahadin, Strahd's chamberlain.
10. The Woman in the Locket: Tamsin Tealeaf lifted a silver locket from a coach with no driver; the woman painted inside calls his name in dreams.

Style samples of existing journal endings (match this voice):

- "Rahadin died before Wren could say Master Sorrel's name to his face. Whatever Sorrel meant his boy to learn at the end went into the ground with the chamberlain."
- "Fen asked Thistle to do it before the moon came, and she did, quickly, the way he taught her."
