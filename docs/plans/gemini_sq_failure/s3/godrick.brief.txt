THE CHARACTER
Sir Godrick Pendlebrook (he/him) is a seven-and-a-half-foot goliath paladin, a squire for twelve years to old Sir Pellam Ashgrove, who on his deathbed knighted him with a soup ladle because he could not lift his sword, then gave him the sword (it had been his grandfather Sir Aldric Ashgrove's, a knight of the dead Order of the Silver Dragon) and said: "Take it to them, lad. Tell them an Ashgrove kept faith. Have them knight you proper." Godrick is big, warm, hearty and sincere; kind first and slow second, never stupid about people, only slow about everything else. Simple words, short declarative sentences, northern English cheer. Pet phrases: "Right then!" "Sir Pellam always said..." "Leave it to me." When he is truly grieving the cheer drops and he speaks very plainly and very gently; those lines should land hard, and he gets one clean line and stops. Never write him cruel, cowardly or mean, and never joke that he is stupid. Secret: he can barely read. Sample lines: "Fear not, madam. I'm a knight. More or less. A ladle was involved." / "Sir Pellam said a knight's only as good as the people he won't leave behind."

THE QUEST: A Knight, More or Less
Godrick carried Sir Aldric's sword to Argynvostholt, the dead Order's ruined manor, to be knighted properly by Sir Godfrey Gwilym, the Order's second, a kind dead knight who forgets things and wrote what mattered on his wall. The party can show Godfrey the sword, read the wall (it says Aldric did not run; he rode for help and the mists closed behind him), and ask Godfrey to knight him. Existing lines for tone:
  Sir Godfrey Gwilym: A ladle. Oh, that's the best thing I've heard in four hundred years. Then you were already a knight, lad. I'm only the witness.
  Godrick: He didn't run. Sir Pellam's grandad didn't run. Sir Pellam carried that his whole life. He never even knew he didn't have to.
  Godrick: Right then. Bed. Big day tomorrow. All the days are big here. Have you noticed? It's very tiring.
  (from the camp talk after a successful knighting) Narrator: Sir Godrick doesn't polish anything tonight. He sits with the ladle in both hands and looks at it a long while.
  Godrick: I keep thinking, I should hang it up somewhere. Over a fireplace. That's what knights do with things. But I haven't got a fireplace. I've got a log.

THE BUG WE ARE FIXING
The party can light the beacon at the top of Argynvostholt's tower, which lets the whole dead Order lie down and go home, Sir Godfrey with them: "In a small room downstairs, under a wall of charcoal names, a big knight in dented plate sits down on his cot and is still." If the party lights it before Godrick has shown Godfrey the sword (or before the wall and the oath are done), Godfrey is gone for good and Godrick can never be knighted. Nothing in the story notices and the quest stays open forever. We are adding a failure ending, stage id "unknighted": the Order went home without him; he keeps the ladle. The party did not do a wicked thing. They lit the dragon's lamp and let the dead rest, which was right. It simply cost Godrick the one thing he came for. This is a quiet camp scene, a few nights after the beacon, with the party's four to six members around the fire.
