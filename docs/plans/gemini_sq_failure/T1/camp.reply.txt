== nelu_away
Narrator: A woman by the lake fire puts a bowl of hot mutton broth into Nelu's hands and pulls a dry wool blanket around his thin shoulders.
Nelu [weary]: They gave me bread and fat, and a coat that smells like somebody else's horse, and they told me the road back to the pool is shut.
Nelu [sad]: Nobody here says why I cannot go home. Have you seen my Da on the road, or is he only looking for me in the wrong part of the trees?
Narrator: The mist off the lake is cold on your face, and the fire snaps in the quiet while the boy waits for an answer you do not have.
Nelu [sad]: That is all right. You pulled me off the wet rock when the water was loud, and that was work enough for one week.
Nelu: When the mist comes up from the water tonight, I am going to sing every song I know until the dark has no room left to talk.

== nelu_away_after
Nelu [weary]: I sit with my back to the lake now, because water sounds the same everywhere once the sun goes down.
Nelu: I do not ask after the pool camp anymore, and if anything calls my name from the reeds, I sing over it until it gives up.

== pen_saved

Narrator: A wet leather purse still hangs from the gatepost where the castle carriage left it, heavy with old gold coins.
Narrator: The mare flattens one ear as you lift the purse from the post, then goes back to chewing the fence.
Narrator: Around you the wagons stand open to the damp air, with nobody left to say whose horse she is now.

== pen_lost

== arrigal_nicu
Narrator: Arrigal listens with his hands folded over the silver buttons of his waistcoat, while the lake wind pulls the smoke sideways.
Arrigal: A boy on the crossroads rope with a thief's board around his neck, and strangers to put him in the dirt when the crows were done.
Arrigal: You did not owe him a spade, and you did not owe me the story. Our people remember who brings word when the roads go bad.
Arrigal: I carry messages, my friend. This one will find his sister and his blood, wherever the wagons have stopped tonight.
Arrigal [weary]: What they do with their grief is their own affair, and none of yours, I think. Sit by the fire until the rain lets up.
