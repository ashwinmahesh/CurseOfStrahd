== vote_call
Narrator: Ostap strikes an iron kettle with a driftwood stick until the fourteen gather round the low, sputtering fires.
Narrator: They wrap themselves in damp wool and watch you with the quiet, unhurried eyes of people who have already been drowned once.
Ostap: Our guests have a boat, and the Baron's men have noticed our smoke. The boat leaks on the left, which is fair enough.
Ostap: They offer to row us back to shore before the watch comes out tonight, or help us pull the reeds over our heads and stay.
== vote_speech
Ostap: Nobody leaves this mud because they are frightened on a dark night. We left Vallaki tied at the wrists, and we will leave here on our own feet or not at all.
Ostap: In town there are roofs that keep the rain off, and dry bread, and neighbours who look at their boots when the guards pass.
Ostap: Out here the perch is bony and the wind comes straight through your coat, but if a man wants to look miserable on a Thursday, nobody hits him with a stick.
Ostap [weary]: Speak if you have a mind to, and then we raise hands. Even the Baron never gave us a vote before a soaking.
== argue_go_ok
Narrator: You speak of winter coming down from the mountains, when the reeds will freeze hard and the lake will skin over with black ice.
Narrator: An old woman rubbing her knuckles by the embers nods once and says, "I left three good pots under my stairs, and my sister's boy has none."
Ostap: The winter is a hard argument to shout down. Well then, let us see the hands.
== argue_go_miss
Narrator: You speak of warm beds and dry floors in town, but a narrow-shouldered cobbler spits into the ash and says, "A dry floor is still the Baron's floor."
Narrator: Around the fire, heads shake slowly in the mist. Their coats are wet, not their spirits.
== argue_stay_ok
Narrator: You tell them that four strokes of mud and an armful of cut sedge will hide this island better than any wall in Vallaki ever hid them.
Narrator: A girl with reed-plaited hair looks toward the dark water and says, "Let them row out for nothing. My feet know this mud now."
Ostap [smile]: Spoken like a proper frog. Let us have the hands, then.
== argue_stay_miss
Narrator: You tell them the reeds will keep them safe if the fires die, but an old dyer coughs into his sleeve and says, "Safe and cold are cousins, friend."
Narrator: Several eyes turn toward the dark line of the shore where the town roofs lie out of the wind.
== vote_go
Ostap: Nine for the shore, five for the reeds. We pack what fits in our pockets and leave the kettles for the crows.
Narrator: Nobody cheers. They tie their bundles with eel-skin twine and kick wet sand over the coals in silence.
== vote_stay
Ostap: Ten for the mud, four for the road. We stay where we are, and tonight we are only frogs and sedge.
Narrator: The fourteen rise without hurry, already reaching for wet sacks and buckets to smother the embers.
== ferry_start
Narrator: Four of the castaways step into Old Nistor's skiff at dusk, settling between the ribs until the gunwales sit three fingers above the black water.
Narrator: "Mind the third plank," whispers a baker with grey knees, "she weeps around the nails."
Narrator: You push off from the shingle, and the reed island vanishes behind you after three strokes.

== seen
Narrator: Across the water, a horn lantern swings up into a bow and holds steady, pointing straight at your wake.

Narrator: Their prow cuts the fog toward the shingle, fast and straight, and there is no hiding the boat now.
== ferried
Narrator: All fourteen stand dripping among the gravel and willow roots by the Vallaki road as the grey morning seeps through the fog.
Narrator: Someone starts an old cellar-song about a barrel that would not hold water, and three more voices take it up before the first verse ends.
Narrator: They walk toward the town gates in their wet boots, singing in plain daylight where the watchmen cannot pretend not to see them.
Ostap [smile]: Fourteen in a bunch is harder to drop in a lake than one at a time. The Baron will have to buy a bigger boat.
Ostap: Come see me if you ever need a cask hooped properly. If I am back in the stocks, knock twice on the timber.
== hide_start
Narrator: You haul lake water in wooden buckets to drown the cooking pits while the castaways pull down their drying lines of gutted perch.
Narrator: Working knee to knee in the dark, you lash cut bundles of sedge over the low willow huts until the roofs match the bank.
Narrator: Fourteen people lie flat in the cold mud beneath the reeds, breathing into their sleeves.
== hide_ok
Narrator: The last steam dies into the mist without a smell of woodsmoke, and from ten paces off the island is only black water and broken rushes.
Narrator: Even the frogs begin to croak again along the shallows.
== hide_bad
Narrator: A knot of pitch-pine deep in the ash refuses to die, sending a thin, stubborn ribbon of grey smoke straight up into the still air.
Narrator: In the marsh silence, the hiss of wet wood sounds loud as a forge.
== boat_passes
Narrator: The Baron's heavy boat grates on the gravel bar, and two guards hold up a lantern that shows nothing but empty sedge and wet stones.
Narrator: "Told you it was marsh-gas," one mutters, and they heave a bound woman over the side into the knee-deep water before pushing off again.
Narrator: As soon as their oar-strokes fade into the fog, four pairs of hands reach out of the reeds to haul her up and cut the cord at her wrists.
Narrator: Someone wraps a dryish sack around her shoulders and says softly, "You are all right now, neighbour. Make it fifteen."
== stay_done
Ostap: Gone back to their warm guardhouse, and left us another neighbour for our trouble.
Ostap [smile]: Fifteen is an awkward number for four huts, mind you. Somebody will have to sleep with their feet out the door.
Ostap: You kept our heads dry tonight, friends. Whenever Vallaki grows too cheerful for your stomach, there is perch on the coals.
