## The scenes

The party goes to the Baron's mansion to get Ireena back. She is in Izek's room, off the great hall: a bare soldier's room with a cot, a washstand with a basin, a sword leaning in a corner, a candle, and an open chest of small carved wooden dolls, every one with the same red-haired face (hers; the toymaker carves them for him). The door to the hall is not locked. Izek's right arm is the scaled, clawed, hot one; his left is ordinary. Ireena has not been hurt: he carried her here, put a doll in her lap and sat watching her. She is angry, very controlled, dry, proud; she does not scream and does not beg; she makes the grim joke before anyone can pity her. She will never say Strahd's name. Izek does NOT hurt her; he wants to keep her safe; with her he goes quiet and almost gentle, which is worse. He does not know she is his sister. Neither does she, unless the party tells them (one branch does: the lines for that moment already exist; see below).
