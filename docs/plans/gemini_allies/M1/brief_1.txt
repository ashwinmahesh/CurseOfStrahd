## The scene

Ezmerelda d'Avenir is a young Vistana monster hunter (long dark coat, rapier, a small crossbow with silver-headed bolts, a spyglass, and an oak-and-steel brace below her right knee where a werewolf took her leg). She was trained by Rudolph van Richten, the legendary monster hunter, who walked into the mists twelve years ago to die alone and has been calling himself Rictavio ever since: a loud half-elf showman in a scarlet coat and a hat of feathers who lodges at the Blue Water Inn in the town of Vallaki and keeps a gaudy crimson carnival wagon in the inn's stable yard that nobody may open. (A great cat is caged in it; the horses in the stable hate it. He hides his real self from the vampire lord Strahd, who would kill him.) Rudolph is really an exhausted, sharp old man with burn-scarred hands who has lost nearly everyone he taught and does not want her to be the next.

The player's party met Ezmerelda camped at Rudolph's tower on Lake Baratok, where his ward (keyed to her alone) had thrown her into the lake. They told her where he was. She said: "Feathers? Tell me there are feathers. No, don't. I'll see for myself. I'm leaving tonight." Now she has come to Vallaki and is waiting in the stable yard at the back of the inn, by his locked wagon, and the party finds her there.

Her fear (the voice bible's secret, never stated flatly): being too late again, and being left behind again. She has not knocked because she is not sure what she will say to him, or whether he wants to be found. She scouted the taproom through the window, saw a man in feathers standing on a chair addressing nobody in particular, and had to sit down. The yard is private (nobody else is in it). The inn is kept by the Martikov family; there are horses in stalls, hay bales, a lantern by the wagon steps, and the wagon's door is padlocked.

Rudolph's room at the inn has a rack of costumes, a washstand with a stage moustache on it, and a lute. In the unmasked version of him he is "low, clipped, precise"; as Rictavio he is bombast and alliteration. He keeps the costume on in public even after the party knows (the town must not suspect); only the voice changes. The wagon's door has a hair across the lock, a habit of his that she would know.

What the lines are for. Nodes, in the order the player meets them:

1. The party enters the yard and Ezmerelda greets them (first time).
2. A menu: bring him out to her / ask why she hasn't gone in / leave.
3. The reunion, when the party brings Rudolph out to her. It has TWO openings (he already knows the party knows who he is; or he does not and arrives in his showman act) and then ONE shared exchange. Her pet test to strangers is "Say something a vampire wouldn't say." Please build the exchange so that she uses that line on him and his answer is the emotional centre (something true and not at all showman-like, such as an apology). It must end like the tower's reunion did: she hits him on the arm, hard, and he lets her. Do not copy the tower's reunion lines (listed below); invent new beats. The great cat in the wagon may react once for a laugh. A variant node is used only if the party had promised to keep his secret and told her anyway: he says so, she takes the blame ("I got it out of them").
4. After the reunion she gives the party a gift (silver-headed bolts, holy water, a healing potion, and a coat of glamoured armour that can look like a ball gown or a beggar's coat). Two lines, neutral about where she is.
5. Later visits to her in the yard: a short hub (how is Rudolph, hunt with us, goodbye) and a joining speech (she joins the party as a companion).
6. Separately: if the showman has already left Vallaki by the time she arrives (he went home to his tower, or he left with the party), she goes back to her camp at Lake Baratok and the party finds her there instead. Short, with a variant.

## Ezmerelda d'Avenir - voice bible

Quick, sardonic, practical; hunter's rules delivered as jokes. Teases, then means it. Calls Van Richten "Rudolph" and treats his legend with exasperated love. Pet phrases: "Say something a vampire wouldn't." "Silver first, questions never." "Stopping's the one thing I'm bad at." Funny through showman timing (she grew up in wagons that put on shows), self-mockery about her own missing parts, and her running complaint about her teacher's habits. Wants: Strahd's end; to find Rudolph and have a word with him first. Fears: being too late again; being left behind again. Secret: she isn't sure she's as brave as her reputation, and has started to think that's wisdom.

Existing Ezmerelda lines (match this register; do not repeat them):

- "Ezmerelda d'Avenir. Vistani, when the wagons will have me. I hunt the things that hunt people. Right now I'm hunting one stubborn old man who taught me how, and who has locked me out of his house."

## The Narrator

An old gothic storyteller, fond of the party the way a gravedigger is fond of the weather. Second person, present tense. One or two sentences; the second is usually the turn (irony, dread, dry joke). Ominous, dryly funny, never cruel. Concrete senses over adjectives (a smell, a sound, a temperature). No game terms: never "roll", "check", "DC", "HP", "level", "skill". No winks at the player. Here the Narrator may be warm: this is a reunion.
Samples:
- "The hall smells of beeswax and old smoke. Somebody kept this house beautifully, right up until they didn't."
- "By a painted wagon, a woman in a long dark coat sits fixing silver heads to crossbow bolts. Below her right knee, a brace of oak and steel; her left hand is bandaged, and she smells faintly of burnt hair."
- "She doesn't look up. She lifts a small loaded crossbow off her knee instead and points it at you."
- "The showman goes out of him like air out of a bladder. What's left is an old man, very tired and very alert, who sits down and lowers his voice."
