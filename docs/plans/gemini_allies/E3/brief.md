## The Narrator

An old gothic storyteller who has watched a great many adventurers walk into Barovia and very few walk out. Not the party's enemy and not its friend; fond of the party the way a gravedigger is fond of the weather: with interest, without illusions. Rules we hold every Narrator line to:

1. Second person, present tense ("You step inside"). Never "the party", never past tense.
2. Dry, ominous, never cruel. The world is cruel; the Narrator isn't. It never gloats and never cheers.
3. Concrete senses over adjectives: a sound, a gesture, a distance, not "tense" or "scary".
4. No modern slang, no winks at the player, and no game terms at all: not "turn", "target", "attack", "combat", "hit points", "initiative", "spell", "roll", "round". Say what happens in the world.
5. Combat lines are fast: ONE sentence, under 15 words. (The single "first time" line below is allowed two sentences, under 30 words in all.)
6. Do not use the character-name placeholders {name} or {target}. Lines are recorded by an actor, so write Ireena's name out when you need it.

## What is changing in play (this is the thing the lines have to carry)

Ireena Kolyana travels with the party as a companion (a "guest"). Until now she fought like a party member and, being a minor noble with about nine hit points, died easily, which made players leave her behind. From now on she keeps out of every fight: when a fight starts she stands back, behind the party, and stays there. She takes no turns. The enemies never attack her and never so much as look at her, and no spell from either side can touch her. The player does not control her and cannot put her in harm's way.

The fiction we want the Narrator to hint at, once, without explaining it: something in this valley has been told whose she is, and goes round her the way water goes round a stone. (Strahd, the vampire lord, has claimed her; she is the woman he has hunted across centuries. The party and the player do NOT know that yet. Never write the name Strahd, never write "vampire", never say who or what claims her; leave it ominous and unexplained.)

Ireena hates being kept back. She is not a damsel and tells anyone who treats her like one that she is not made of glass. She is tall, red-haired, in her early twenties, proud, direct and dry, and she keeps a sword by the fire and knows how to use it. The Narrator is fond of her and doesn't pity her. Her grief shows as precision, her fear as a straight back.
