## The scene

Ireena Kolyana is a young woman in her early twenties, red-haired, the adopted daughter of the late burgomaster Kolyan Indirovich of the Village of Barovia. The vampire lord of the valley has come to her window twice; twice she woke with two punctures on her neck and no memory of letting him in. The player's party buried her father with her in the village churchyard, and then took her somewhere safe and left her there (or she travels with them). In the game she can no longer fight: something in the valley goes round her like water round a stone, and she hates being kept back from it. Wherever she is, the player can walk up to her and have a "check-in" conversation. This errand is one of the things she can ask for in it.

THE ERRAND, "Flowers for Father". Ireena is staying somewhere safe far from the village (the Blue Water Inn or St. Andral's church in Vallaki, or the burgomaster's house in Krezk; the lines must work at any of them, so NEVER name the place she is in). The road back to the village is too dangerous for her to walk alone, and she will not drag the party off a road for her. She asks the party, the next time they are in the village, to lay a pressed sprig on Kolyan's grave in the churchyard and to tell her how it looks. The sprig is something small and dry that she pressed in a book the night of the burial and has kept since (choose a hardy herb, rosemary or similar; do not make it a flower garden). At the grave, the party lays it down: there is a short scene. Later the party reports back to her, truthfully or with a kind lie, and she tells them ONE story about her father that she has never told anyone. That is the whole errand. It is quiet. There are no fights, no checks and no rewards in the lines themselves.

What the grave looks like in the game (already written, do not repeat the exact words): fresh earth, a board with his name burned into it, and someone's wildflowers already gone brown. The churchyard has a raven on a stone and a gravedigger working at the far end. Her father was burgomaster of the village for twenty years, a stubborn old man, dead of a failed heart at his own front door after a month of wolves at the shutters. He found her as a small girl wandering on the lake road, red hair full of frost, bare feet, and no name she would give him; he wrapped her in his coat and raised her. (That is already told in the game, so do not retell it, and do NOT tell the story of how she got her name.) Her brother Ismark, the old man's son, is a swordsman who drinks too much and uses gallows humour; he may or may not still be in the village.

## Ireena Kolyana - voice bible

Direct and proud. Short sentences, no ornament. She asks questions like a magistrate. Dry humor with an edge; she makes the grim joke before anyone can pity her. Never says "please" to the devil. Calls Strahd "him" or "the devil" or "our visitor" and NEVER says his name. Gets more formal, not softer, when she is grieving; grief comes out as precision (about the funeral, the paperwork, the exact order of things). Her pet phrases are "I'm not made of glass." "Say it plainly." "Father would want it done properly." (She is NOT glass: do not make her fragile; use the glass phrase at most once in the whole reply, or not at all.) She judges people by how they treat her father's memory. She hates being kept out of things. Wants: to bury her father properly, then to stop being prey; she would rather be hunted on a road than waited for in a parlour. Fears: the dreams, a lake, a white dress, a fall; that one night she will open the window herself. Secret (never stated flatly here): some nights she does not want to keep the window shut.

Her existing lines (match this register; do not repeat them):
- "I'm not made of glass. I'm made of whatever's left when the glass breaks. Now, are you here to help carry him?"
- "Father would want it done properly: the priest, the words, the earth. After that, take me anywhere he isn't."
- "None of you were priests when we buried Father, and the plain words you said over the dirt suited him better."
- "Father always took an honest sentence over a pretty one, and I judge you kindly for giving him that."
- "Every name-day I can remember, Father bought one small cask from the Wizard of Wines and tapped it by the fire. It tasted of sour plums and damp cellar oak, and I thought it was the finest thing in the world."
- "I sit in Father's chair after the lamps go out and talk to the panelling. The house is quieter when someone speaks to it."
- "Back with all your limbs. Good. Tell me what you stirred up out there."
- "Is anyone hurt? Say it plainly. I'd rather know now than at the next door."
- "I keep a scarf over the marks. I'm not ashamed of them. I'm ashamed I don't remember."

Ismark Kolyanovich (her brother, a swordsman in his late twenties, broad in the shoulder and hollow under the eyes): weary gallows wit, short plain sentences then one dry sting at the end; calls Strahd "the devil" or "his lordship". His lines: "They call me Ismark the Lesser. Fair enough. My father was the greater, and look where it got him." / "I'd die for her. I'd prefer to do something more useful first." He is proud of her and cannot say so.

Kolyan Indirovich, the dead burgomaster: blunt, loud, impatient, a man interrupted halfway through an argument he meant to win. His lines: "Twenty years I kept this village. Two weeks dead and you lot are digging me up." / "I know who you are, girl. I'm dead, not blind."

The Narrator: an old gothic storyteller. Second person, present tense, one or two sentences, the second is usually the turn. Ominous, dryly funny, never cruel. Concrete senses over adjectives (a smell, a sound, a temperature). Respect the dead. No game terms (no "check", "roll", "level").
