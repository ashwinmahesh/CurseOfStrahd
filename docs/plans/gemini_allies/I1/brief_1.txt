## The scene

Ireena Kolyana is a young woman in her early twenties, red-haired, the adopted daughter of the late burgomaster Kolyan Indirovich of the Village of Barovia. The vampire lord of the valley has come to her window twice; twice she woke with two punctures on her neck and no memory of letting him in. The player's party buried her father with her, and then either took her somewhere safe and left her there, or it travels with her. In the game she can no longer fight: something in the valley goes round her like water round a stone, and she hates being kept back from it.

This conversation is a "check-in": the player walks up to her, picks "How are you holding up?" and she talks about her life where she is, tells the party her reactions to what they have been doing, and sometimes raises something that weighs on her. It is NOT a quest and nothing in it is a choice with consequences; it is a way for the player to feel that she is a person who is still there, still thinking, still dry and proud, while they are out in the valley. She greets the party differently the first time, the second and later. She hears the news the party's deeds make. Each check-in she is in one of five situations:

1. THE BLUE WATER INN, Vallaki. The Martikov family keep it; the innkeeper Urwin Martikov sits up all night watching the windows. (Already said, so do not repeat: "The Martikovs are kind. Urwin sits up all night watching the windows. I've never met a man so interested in birds." Behind it: the Martikovs are secretly the Keepers of the Feather, wereravens who watch the valley. She has no proof and a growing suspicion; if the party earned the Keepers' trust, she has worked it out.)
2. ST. ANDRAL'S CHURCH, Vallaki. Holy ground since the saint's bones were brought home. Father Lucian is the priest. (Already said, so do not repeat: "Father Lucian's teaching me the long prayers. I'm terrible at them. He says the saint doesn't mind." and "I slept through the night. The whole night. I'd forgotten that was a thing people did.")
3. KREZK, at the house of the burgomaster Dmitri Krezkov and his wife Anna, behind the last wall on the road. Their small son Ilya has been very ill. There is a pool in the town that never freezes. (Already said, so do not repeat: "Anna's teaching me to make cheese. I'm terrible at it. Dmitri says it builds character. I have character. I'd like a sword drill." and "The boy's well. He follows me round asking if I've seen the sun.")
4. THE BURGOMASTER'S MANSION, in her home village, if the party would not take her away. Shutters nailed shut every night, wolves at the boards, her brother Ismark (Kolyan's son, her adoptive brother, nicknamed "the Lesser", a swordsman who drinks at the tavern in the village) bringing food and standing at doors.
5. ON THE ROAD with the party (she is a travelling companion, in whatever place the party happens to be). Do not name a place.

## Ireena Kolyana - voice bible

Direct and proud. Short sentences, no ornament. She asks questions like a magistrate. Dry humor with an edge; she makes the grim joke before anyone can pity her. Never says "please" to the devil. Calls Strahd "him" or "the devil" or "our visitor" and NEVER says his name. Gets more formal, not softer, when she is grieving; grief comes out as precision. Her pet phrases are "I'm not made of glass." "Say it plainly." "Father would want it done properly." (She is NOT glass: do not make her fragile; use the glass phrase at most once in the whole reply, or not at all.) She judges people by how they treat her father's memory. She hates being kept out of things. Wants: to bury her father properly, then to stop being prey; she would rather be hunted on a road than waited for in a parlour. Fears: the dreams, a lake, a white dress, a fall; that one night she will open the window herself. Secret (never stated flatly): some nights she does not want to keep the window shut.

Her existing lines (match this register; do not repeat them):

- "He's been to my window twice. I don't remember opening it either time. That's the part that frightens me."

- "Last night you stood in my father's house, and nobody ran. I've watched a lot of people run. I won't forget it."
- "I keep a scarf over the marks. I'm not ashamed of them. I'm ashamed I don't remember."

- "Is anyone hurt? Say it plainly. I'd rather know now than at the next door."
