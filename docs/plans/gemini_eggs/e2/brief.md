## Who is speaking: the Narrator

An old gothic storyteller who has watched a great many adventurers walk into the valley and very few walk out. Not the party's enemy and not its friend. Fond of the party the way a gravedigger is fond of the weather: with interest, without illusions. Never gloats over a failure, never cheers a success. It notices.

The Narrator's rules (from our voice guide):

1. Second person, present tense ("You step inside."). When a line is about one character it may use the placeholder {name} (that character's first name), e.g. "{name} catches the glint first."
2. One or two sentences. The second sentence is usually the turn: the irony, the dread, the joke.
3. Ominous, dryly funny, never cruel. Laugh at the house, the dark, the situation, never at the player for a bad roll.
4. No modern slang, no winks at the player, no game terms. No "attempt", "roll", "check", "level", "skill", "try number five", "fail". Dice results are described as what happened in the world.
5. Concrete senses over adjectives (a sound, a smell, a temperature, a small physical detail).
6. Never tells the player what to feel. Never uses "suddenly". Never explains a joke. Never comments on game systems.

## The scene logic

In the game a locked door can be picked, one try at a time. Each failed try already gets a short line ("The lock holds."). We are adding two rare, one-per-playthrough lines about a lock that will not give:

1. STUBBORN: it plays on the fifth failed try at the same lock. The Narrator notes, in the world and without counting aloud, that this lock has stopped merely resisting and has started to enjoy itself. It must work at any lock in the valley: a farmhouse door, a cellar hatch, a chest in a crypt. Do not name a place, a building, a person or a tool. It may mention the lock, the pins, the latch, the keyhole, the hinges, the cold, the fog, the wood or the iron.
2. AT_LAST: it plays when that same lock finally gives, after at least five failures. The lock gives with dignity: like something that held out as long as honour required and is now glad it is over. No cheering, no "finally you did it", no congratulations; the Narrator only notices.
