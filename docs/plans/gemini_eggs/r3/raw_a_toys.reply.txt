1A: Narrator: High on the top board, behind a tin drum, a pale wooden dandy in a red-lined coat poses before a thumb-sized looking glass, his silver-white curls combed neat. Turn him how you like; the glass gives back only the dusty rafters and the shop behind his shoulder.
1B: Narrator: Up near the ceiling sits a puppet with painted ivory skin, silver-white hair, and a velvet collar turned up against the draft, admiring himself in a little pewter mirror. The mirror looks straight through his wooden chest and shows only the shelves behind him.
2A: Narrator: Your eyes find nothing up there but gray wool dust, a spider's empty pantry, and a dented tin drum with no sticks.
2B: Narrator: Nothing waits on the high board save a skin of old dust, a sagging cobweb, and a rusted tin drum.
3A: Emanoil [smile]: Look at the curl of that lip, the tragic cut of the coat—he is the very image of the cursed nobility! | Emanoil [neutral]: Take twice what the toymaker asked, I insist, even if I must live on turnip broth till Tuesday. | Emanoil [smile]: "O pale little gentleman, carved out of pine, / The glass keeps its secrets, and you shall keep mine!"
3B: Emanoil [smile]: A pale gentleman with an empty looking glass—why, he has been waiting in that dust for a soul who understands him! | Emanoil [weary]: Here, take double the coppers, take my ink-money too, before I remember I owe my landlady for candles. | Emanoil [smile]: "Though painted and posed in thy crimson-lined coat, / The mirror sees naught of thy cold wooden throat!"
