1A: Baron [smile]: Hear ye! By order of the Burgomaster, no citizen shall shove a neighbor off a roof, into the river, or onto a festival bonfire, however festive the spirit of the push! | Baron: We shall keep both feet on the cobbles, cheerfully, until further notice!
1B: Baron [smile]: Notice to all citizens! From this hour forth, any merrymaker who tumbles a fellow townsman from a height, a bridge, or into the celebratory flames shall be placed in the stocks! | Baron: High spirits are mandatory, yes, yes—gravity is not! All will be well!
2A: Narrator: You scratch behind his ears until his hind leg starts to thump against the cobbles. Around the square, three citizens in painted smiles stop to watch a creature enjoy something without being told to.
2B: Narrator: Your hand finds the matted spot behind his collar, and the big dog leans his whole weight against your shin with a long, whistling sigh. It is the only honest sound in the square.
3A: Narrator: You rub the coarse fur between his shoulders; he gives your knuckles one polite lick for the effort, then turns his nose back toward the bubbling pot.
3B: Narrator: He accepts your scratching with a brief, businesslike wag of his tail, though his eyes never once leave the ladle resting on the rim of the stew pot.
4A: Narrator: Weeks of rain-blurred chalk show a masked swordsman driving goblins from a little farmstead, one cobblestone at a time. In the freshest corner, a steadier hand has given the hero two curved horns, an eyepatch, and a tall lady in red holding his leash.
4B: Narrator: Across the damp stones, a chalk-drawn swordsman defends a crooked barn from goblin raiders in a tale added to day by day. Where the dry chalk ends, someone has drawn little horns on his brow, a patch over one eye, and a leash leading up to a tall woman in red.
