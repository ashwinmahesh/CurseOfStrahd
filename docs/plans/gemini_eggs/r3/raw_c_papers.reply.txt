1A: Child, if you think a painted wagon and a loud coat will fool the dark, your wits have gone the way of your knees. Stop chasing monsters alone through every wet ditch you can find. Your letters smell of tallow and salt pork; eat something green before your remaining teeth mutiny, and write back so I know which fog swallowed you. As for me, I am still not dead, despite everyone's dedicated efforts. [a little harp and a crescent moon]
1B: You are far too old to be sleeping in boots, child, and far too stubborn to die somewhere convenient. Stop chasing monsters alone as though the night owes you a debt only your stiff back can collect. Put down the silver, eat something green that did not grow in a cellar, and write back before snow closes the passes. I remain stubbornly not dead myself, despite everyone's efforts and a very rude winter. [a little harp and a crescent moon]
2A: Memory is a splinter the bright sister leaves in the flesh; only the dark sister draws it out. Come into the unlit room and lay your griefs upon her cold palm, for the night keeps what it takes, and in the mercy of forgetting, no wound can ache twice. In the margin: The dark-haired novice kept forgetting her own name, and would not stop picking the white flowers that open only at night; we had to take her shears away twice, and still found petals in her sleeves at matins.
2B: Light asks you to carry every dead thing you have ever loved; the dark sister asks only that you set the burden down. Give your worn names to the quiet dark, where the night keeps what it takes and the comfort of forgetting folds over the spirit like snow. In the margin: The youngest girl kept forgetting her own name, and would not stop picking the white flowers that open only at night; we had to take her shears away twice before locking the garden gate.
