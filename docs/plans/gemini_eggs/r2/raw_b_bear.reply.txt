1A: Druid [smile]: If the planks can bear two of us, I could put on the pelt and take the second turn beside him. He has a very fine shoulder for a waltz.
1B: Druid [sly]: Should he ever want a partner of his own weight on the boards, I know the shape well enough. I promise not to step on his claws.
2A: Lavinia [smile]: Easy now, he is spoken for—my hand has been on his collar since he was the size of that cask. You may have the third dance, if you wipe your paws.
2B: Lavinia [smile]: Hold your pelt, friend, I am still his first partner on these planks. Though if you bring honey, he might let you cut in after the tambourine.
3A: Druid [weary]: A den is cold with only one great back to keep the wind out. In the brown coat, I could go up the hill and see how he keeps house.
3B: Druid [smile]: Come the first frost, I might borrow four paws and call on him in the dark under the roots. A bear ought to have company that understands the winter.
4A: Lavinia [sad]: Go on then, and tell him his girl sent you with her love. Only scrape your claws at the stone before you go in—he is particular about his moss.
4B: Lavinia [smile]: Give the old fool a cuff behind the ear from me if you do. And mind your manners at the threshold; it is his roof now, not mine.
