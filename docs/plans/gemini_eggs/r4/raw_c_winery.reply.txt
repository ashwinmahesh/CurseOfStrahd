1A: TO WHOEVER STACKS LAMP OIL AGAINST THE GREAT PRESS. This roof covers dry oak, old straw, and a hundred casks of spirit. Even the walking kindling on the hill knows what oil does to wood, and my house will not be outthought by a bush. Move it. —Davian Martikov
1B: TO THE HANDS LEAVING OIL CASKS BY THE PRESS WALL. A press house is dry timber and raw spirit, not a bonfire waiting for an excuse. Even the walking thorns in the rows have sense enough to fear a lamp. Clear the wall before dusk. Those are the terms. Hm.

2A line two: With a dry creak of bark, the little creature draws itself up taller, its lone leaf standing bolt upright while a root-tip wags in the dirt.
2B line one: You rest your hand briefly on the knot of thorns, smoothing the dry bark from crown to shoulder.
