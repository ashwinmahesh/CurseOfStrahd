1A: My dearest Morgantha,
Send back the scone receipt by the next crow—the good one, with the marrow-salt. You’ve had it since autumn, and my tea-cups do not wash themselves. The parlour has been wonderfully full since the rains; three new guests took a fancy to the place and decided to stay for good. They sit by the hearth and never make a crumb of mess. Quite my favourite arrangement.
Mind your ovens, petal.
Your loving aunt, by the river

1B: Dearest Morgantha,
Do return my scone receipt when you’ve copied it—the company one, with the ground teeth. A river tea-house takes keeping up, and the saucers don't rinse themselves. You would envy my parlour this season; four travellers came in out of the bog and stayed for good. They sit where I put them, quiet as china, and never ask for a second pot. I have grown quite fond of the arrangement.
Keep warm, sweetling.
Your loving aunt, by the river

2A: Pinned behind the tallow-stained ledger by a rusted needle is a folded sheet of river-reed paper, written in a brisk, looping hand that belongs to none of the three.

2B: Tucked behind the final reckoning, abrass pin holds a damp-curled letter in a fourth hand, older and neater than the others, smelling faintly of bog-myrtle and boiled kettle.

3A: The last leaves of the book are welded shut with old suet and brown sugar, and pull apart only into illegible grey shreds.

3B: Whatever was written at the back has been drowned in spilt lard and plum-grease, leaving nothing but a sweet, rancid smear.

4A: Tangled in the severed hemp are a scrap of tiny leather harness and a pair of bent brass spectacles. Directly below, the gorse is crushed flat, and no footprints stay.

4B: Someone small was lashed to the spar once, and left behind a sliced strap and a pair of miniature brass spectacles. Far below, a single hole in the brambles leads away down the hill.

5A: A severed harness strap, and brass spectacles pinched at the bridge for a gnome's nose. Whoever wore them went down through the gorse below, fast, and kept running.

5B: You turn the tiny brass spectacles in your palm; the ear-wires are bent to a gnome's measure. The cut rope and the flattened brambles below say their owner took the short way down, and walked away.

6A: The cut leather strap is small, and the bent brass spectacles are narrower still—a halfling's fit, or near enough to chill the blood. Below the sail, a crushed bush and a quick, light trail vanish into the heather.

6B: You know the span of those tiny brass frames; they were made for a halfling's brow. The cords here were sliced clean through, and the broken gorse below shows only that whoever fell didn't stay to look for their glasses.
