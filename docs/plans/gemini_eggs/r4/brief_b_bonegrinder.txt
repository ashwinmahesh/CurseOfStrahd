PART 1: A LETTER IN A HAG'S RECEIPT BOOK
Place: Old Bonegrinder, a windmill on a hill where a family of hags (Morgantha, a night hag who sells "dream pastries" door to door, and her daughters) bake pastries and keep village children in the loft. On a table there is Morgantha's receipt book, in three hands. If the party looks hard at the back pages (a careful search), they find a fourth, older letter pinned behind the ledger, from Morgantha's aunt: a GREEN HAG who keeps a tea-house by a river far off. The letter shows the family: horror-comic, never cosy. Contents of the letter: the aunt wants the scone receipt she lent her niece returned (it is the good one, with the extra ingredient, and the cups do not wash themselves); she writes affectionately and boasts about the guests who "stayed for good" (they now sit in her parlour and never leave, she has grown fond of the arrangement). She signs herself only "your loving aunt, by the river". She should NOT use the name Ethel or the word Auntie as a name.
