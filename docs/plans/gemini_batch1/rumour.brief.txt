Reply with plain text only (no JSON, no markdown tables, no code fences) and do not send messages to any other agent. Write only what is asked, with no commentary before or after.

TONE: gothic horror with dry, understated, character-driven humour. The village people are tired, frightened, poor and wry; they are never silly and never wink at the player. No modern slang, no pop culture, no real-world references. Funny comes from specifics and deadpan, not from jokes about the horror. Where a scene is sad, let it be sad.

OUTPUT FORMAT: for each label below write the label on its own line (like "== nothing_new_1"), then each line on its own line as `Speaker: text` or `Speaker [mood]: text`. Speakers are exactly the names given. Do not number the lines. Moods are one of: neutral, smile, angry, afraid, sad, sly, weary; use a mood only where it matters.

RULES FOR EVERY LINE: under 190 characters, usually 60 to 130; no braces or placeholders; no em dashes (use commas, full stops or colons); no real-world references; straight apostrophes and quotes; each line should make sense on its own. The Narrator speaks in second person, present tense, one or two sentences. Never name the player characters.

CHARACTERS: Vasile and Petre are two threadbare villagers who stand outside the tavern trading the same three rumours "in turns". Vasile is the dramatist, Petre the contradictor. Arik is the barkeep who speaks one word at a time. Goodwife Petra is the careful grey well-woman. Bildrath is the shopkeeper: no credit, no returns, ten times the going price, never gives a discount, precise and humourless.

THE SCENE (a gentle comic side quest, no fight): the party offers Vasile and Petre a fourth rumour, which the village has not had in years, and sells it to them. The party picks one of three rumours to plant: that the Baron of Vallaki is bald under his wig; that the burgomaster's old wolfhound Lupu can count; that Bildrath gives discounts on Tuesdays. The rumour spreads and comes back garbled: Arik's one-word version, and Goodwife Petra's garbled version. If the Tuesday rumour is believed, Bildrath, out of spite, honours it once. Playing it straight is the whole joke.
