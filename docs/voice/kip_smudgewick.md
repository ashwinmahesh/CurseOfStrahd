## Who he is

Thirty-one, maroon-skinned, curly-horned, in a straw hat. The Smudgewicks have grown apricots on the same hillside
for six generations, and Kip has driven the cart to market since he was nine: apricots, apricot jam, dried apricots,
apricot brandy, pruning knives, grafting wax, straw hats, ladders and "the finest apricot-pit charms in three
counties" (he carves them himself). He is the best salesman in the family and the worst liar.

Two summers ago a blight took the orchard. Trees died in rows. His mother had borrowed against the land, his little
sister Pip was sick that winter, and there was no money for the medicine or the debt. A gentleman in a grey coat
came to the farm gate, very polite, very patient, and introduced himself as **Mister Quillon**, an assessor. Kip's
father sent him away. Kip went after him, alone, and signed.

The orchard lived. The debt was paid. Pip got better. And the apricots have never rotted since, not one, not ever,
which is how his family knows something is wrong, though they don't know what. The contract's term runs out at the
end of the "final harvest". Mister Quillon has offered him one way out: bring back a signature from the lord of a
land of mists.

(Writers: Quillon means a soul. The contract says only "a signature of the lord of the land of mists, freely given
in his own hand". Strahd signs his dinner invitation. Master Vosk, the arcanaloth broker in the Amber Temple, can
read the fine print for a fee. Kip's quest, `the_fine_print`, is in docs/story/personal_quests.md.)

- **Wants:** out of the contract, with the orchard safe and his family none the wiser.
- **Fears:** the Hells. His family finding out. Becoming the cheat his horns tell everyone he is.
- **Secret:** his father refused the devil first, and Kip signed behind his back. He has never told anyone.

## How he talks

- Fast, friendly, eager-to-please salesman patter in a soft West Country accent. "Friend." "Neighbour." "No
  obligation."
- Always selling, especially when nervous: "Apricot? First one's free. Second one's also free, I'm a bit anxious."
- Lapses into contract language when stressed: "hereinafter", "the party of the first part", "notwithstanding".
- Apologises a lot. Honest to a fault in small things, which makes the big secret worse.
- Pet phrases: "No obligation!" "Read the small print, that's what Mum says." "Fresh as the day they were picked.
  Which is the problem." "I'm sure it's fine."
- When frightened, he talks faster and sells harder. When he is truly cornered, he goes slow, polite and hard, like
  a man reading out terms.

## What makes him funny

A nervous wreck with excellent customer service. He tries to sell apricots to ghosts, hags and vampire spawn, and
sometimes succeeds. He is the most anxious person in the party and makes the bravest decisions in it.

## Reactions

- **Godrick:** his best customer; Godrick has offered to fight the devil, twice, which Kip finds both moving and
  legally inadvisable.
- **Liriel:** wants to save his soul. He's touched, awkward and a little scared of her, and keeps giving her apricots
  as if that settles it.
- **Thistle:** she sniffs him and says he smells like smoke and fruit. He has started carrying a lavender sachet.
- **Ratatoille:** apricot tarts, apricot glaze, a shop together someday. Rat reads the contract like a recipe and
  finds the missing ingredient.
- **Wren:** a double act. Wren helps him sell and helps him look for loopholes.
- **Mister Quillon:** courteous terror. He always says "Mister Quillon", never just "Quillon".
- **Vosk:** recognises a professional at once, and is more afraid of the jackal than of anything else in the temple.
- **Strahd:** "Lovely to meet you, sir, your lordship, sir. Would you sign something for me? No reason."
- **Barovian villagers:** gives fruit to hungry children for free, and is quietly heartbroken by a land with no
  orchards.

## Rules for writers

- Nervous, never spineless. He made a terrible bargain for a good reason and he owns it.
- The apricots are his comfort and his curse: funny in a market, sinister when they don't rot.
- He never jokes about his family.

## Sample lines

- "Apricot? Fresh as the day they were picked. Which was in spring. Of last year. Don't think about it."
- "Says here 'the party of the first part'. That's me. I'm the party of the first part. I hate being the party of the
  first part."
- "Sir, your lordship, sir, would you sign this? It's a, uh, a receipt."

## For casting

A friendly, anxious tiefling man in his early thirties with a warm, quick, slightly breathless baritone and a soft
West Country English accent. A fast-talking market-stall salesman, polite and eager to please, apologising and
selling at once; slow, careful and hard as nails when he is cornered.
