# Silvain Aster — voice bible

High Elf Wizard (Evoker), Sage. Pregen `silvain_aster`, they/them. Identity tags: `bookish`, `proud`,
`secretly kind` (the last has a space and can't be used as a selector). Selectors for writers:
`interject name:silvain_aster:` for their own voice; `class:wizard`, `background:sage`, `species:elf`,
`tag:bookish`, `tag:proud` for lines any such character could say.

## Who they are

A scholar of a hundred and forty years who has read more than most libraries hold and published less than they would
like. They are hunting a book rumored to be written in a vampire's own hand. Its last known owner, a colleague named
Aurel Mirescu, walked into the mists after it and wrote home exactly once: *He knows I am reading.* Silvain tells
people they want the book for the scholarship. They also want to know what happened to Aurel, who once lent them a
pen and never asked for it back.

(Writers: the book is the Tome of Strahd. Silvain should light up at any library, journal or letter, and go cold at
any sign that Strahd reads what people write.)

- **Wants:** the book, the truth about Aurel, and to be recognized as the person who found both.
- **Fears:** being ordinary. And, newly, knowledge that cannot be unread.
- **Secret:** they are kind, constantly, and always pretend it's for a practical reason.

## How they talk

- Precise, elaborate, slightly formal. Complete sentences under any circumstances, including fire.
- Corrects people. Cannot help it. Corrects themself too, which is rarer and funnier.
- Academic framing for horror: footnotes, citations, "the literature suggests".
- Elven perspective: references to things that happened before anyone else was born, offered as small talk.
- Pet phrases: "Technically..." "Fascinating. Horrible, but fascinating." "Do not touch that." "I'll want notes."
- When frightened, they get more precise, not less. A Silvain listing exact measurements is a Silvain about to panic.

## What makes them funny

Pedantry under pressure, and kindness disguised as logic ("I'm only giving you my cloak because a shivering archer
misses"). They are proud, and the world keeps proving them right in the worst way.

## Reactions

- **Fighters:** "Ilse, the short version is: it's dead, it's angry, hit it." They admire competence they lack.
- **Rogues:** delighted by Tamsin's curiosity, horrified by his methods, secretly teaching him cantrips.
- **Clerics:** spirited theological debate with Hedda, conducted with deep mutual respect.
- **Other wizards and scholars:** competitive, then generous.
- **Books, letters, inscriptions:** they want to read everything first. Writers should give them the lore lines.
- **Children:** awkward, formal, and very gentle. Explains things to children as if to colleagues, which children love.
- **Strahd:** an intellectual equal they would very much like to be wrong about.
- **Party reputation:** pretends not to care what anyone thinks. Remembers every kindness the party did.

## Rules for writers

- Silvain uses they/them. Other characters refer to them as "they" (and Silvain will correct anyone who doesn't).
- Give them big words, but keep the line short. One clause of lecture, then the point.
- They never say "I care". They say "this is the logical course of action" and then do the kind thing.

## Sample lines

- "Fascinating. Horrible, but fascinating. Do not touch that, Tamsin. I said do not. Thank you."
- "Technically, a house cannot be hungry. I would like that noted before it eats us."
- "Here, take my cloak. A shivering child is a distraction, and I can't afford distractions. That's all it is."
