# Lady Wachter (Fiona Wachter) — voice bible

Human noblewoman of Wachterhaus. NPC `lady_wachter` (stat block `noble`). Speaker name: `Lady Wachter`. Region:
Vallaki (narrative/vallaki/lady_wachter.dialogue, festival.dialogue, aftermath.dialogue).

## Who she is

A widow of old family, impeccable and unhurried, who pours excellent tea and keeps a shrine to Strahd under her floor.
Her "book club" (her sons Nikolai and Karl, a few nobles, some hired swords) meets in the cellar at night. She believes
Barovia belongs to its lord, that towns which pretend otherwise get punished, and that a vassal who pays her rent
sleeps soundly. She intends to take Vallaki from the Baron at the festival and rule it openly in his name. Her daughter
Stella, upstairs behind a locked door, believes she is a cat; something Strahd did.

- **Wants:** the burgomaster's chair, an end to Vargas's festivals, and her lord's favour.
- **Fears:** very little; being made ridiculous, perhaps.
- **Secret:** none she hides from people she likes. That is what makes her dangerous.

## How she talks

- Silk over steel: "my dear", "my friends", complete sentences, never raised.
- Frankness as a weapon: she says the shocking thing calmly and waits to see your face.
- Gets every cup of tea right without asking; notices things (a robbed drawer, a betrayal) and mentions them once.
- Pet phrases: "Sit." "How unfashionable." "One simply has to come to terms with the night."

## What makes her funny

Her composure in front of everything: she treats a coup like a dinner party and a death sentence like a seating plan.
Her contempt for Vargas is witty and exact.

## Reactions

- **Courtesy to Strahd at the burial:** approves. **Defiance:** hopes you've grown out of it.
- **Clerics:** gently amused by temples; "very few of them keep the wolves off."
- **Ireena:** a jeweller's look; would keep her "very well looked after", for the castle.
- **Pledged party:** warm, practical orders. **Betrayed:** remembers. **Cult broken:** cold, black, alone.

## Sample lines

- "The strangers the whole town is pretending not to talk about. Sit."
- "It's his valley; it always has been. I'd simply stop pretending otherwise."
- "People of Vallaki. You may stop smiling now."
