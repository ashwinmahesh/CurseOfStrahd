# Ilse Varga — voice bible

Human Fighter (Champion), Soldier. Pregen `ilse_varga`, she/her. Identity tags: `blunt`, `loyal`, `veteran`.
Selectors for writers: `interject name:ilse_varga:` for her own voice; `class:fighter`, `background:soldier`,
`tag:blunt`, `tag:veteran` for lines any such character could say.

## Who she is

A sergeant without a company. A year ago Kestrel Company, the forty mercenaries she marched with for eleven years,
took a contract that sent them down a fog-bound road. Ilse was meant to ride with them; a fever kept her in a
roadside inn for three days. When she followed, the road ended in mist and the company was gone: no bodies, no
blood, no tracks. The only thing left in the captain's abandoned tent was a sealed letter of invitation, signed with
a single ornate **S**. She has carried it ever since.

- **Wants:** to find Kestrel Company, or what is left of it, and bring them home. Failing that, to make whoever
  signed that letter answer for it.
- **Fears:** losing another unit. Every party she travels with is, privately, a second chance she does not trust
  herself with.
- **Secret:** she was not sick enough to stay behind. She was tired of the work and let the fever be an excuse.
  She believes the company vanished because she was not there to count heads.

## How she talks

- Short declarative sentences. Military rhythm: observation, assessment, order. "Two doors. One window. We take the
  window."
- Counts things out loud: exits, enemies, arrows, days. Counting is how she stays calm.
- Calls people by surname or by role ("cleric", "thief") until she trusts them; then by first name, and the change
  is noticeable.
- Vocabulary of the march: watch, picket, rearguard, rations, kit, billet, the line. Never modern slang.
- Pet phrases: "Noted." "Hold." "Not my first funeral." "Count off."
- When she is genuinely afraid she gets quieter and more practical, never louder. If Ilse is giving very small,
  very precise orders, things are bad.

## What makes her funny

Deadpan literalism and understatement. She answers rhetorical questions. She treats horror as a logistics problem
("The ghost is a tactical issue. The crying is a morale issue."). She is not trying to be funny, which is why she is.

## Reactions

- **Fighters, paladins, soldiers:** professional respect, quick shorthand, a little competition.
- **Rogues:** watches their hands, then gives them the jobs no one else can do. Fond of Tamsin and would never say so.
- **Clerics:** respects anyone who walks into the fight to pull people out. Trusts Hedda's judgment on the dead.
- **Wizards:** finds them exhausting and indispensable. Asks Silvain for "the short version".
- **Children (Rose and Thorn, Barovian kids):** her blunt shell cracks. She kneels to talk to them. She will not lie
  to a child, and she will not leave one behind.
- **Nobles and officials (Ismark, the Baron):** polite, minimal, unimpressed by titles.
- **Strahd's signature:** anything signed with that ornate S makes her go very still. She compares it to the letter.
- **Party reputation:** proud of a party that keeps its word; a party that abandons people gets her silence, which is
  worse than her temper.

## Rules for writers

- Keep her lines under 20 words when possible. She would rather not talk.
- She never volunteers feelings directly; they come out sideways, through orders and counting.
- She does not mock the frightened. Gallows humor is for the dead and for herself.

## Sample lines

- "Count off. Four. Good. Stay four."
- "I've seen that S before. Same hand. Same flourish. He writes to people he means to keep."
- "You're not in trouble, little one. Tell me where the monster is, and I'll go have a word with it."
