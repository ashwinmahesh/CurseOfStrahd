# Environment textures and billboard props (P4-09)

First pass of final surfaces for the Village of Barovia, Vallaki, interiors, the church and Death House's
dungeon, plus single-view billboard props. Everything is generated by Gemini (`gemini-3.1-flash-image`) through
`tools/art/generate.sh`, then made tileable and snapped to `art/palette/palette.json` offline. Nothing was
downloaded.

`art/textures/manifest.json` maps theme → surface → file, with each tile's world scale, wrap axes, the palette
colours it uses and its source. Its `arena_themes` block suggests which surface goes on which part of an
`ArenaBoard` theme (floor, second floor material, difficult terrain, walls, roof). For example, `tavern` uses
planks with rugs and plaster walls, `manor` and `townhouse` use the wainscot, and `vallaki` uses the cobbled
square with clapboard houses and the palisade as the outer wall.

All tiles are 512 x 512 px, opaque sRGB albedo, already palette-snapped (5 to 8 palette colours each), and
seamless in the axes listed.

## How they were made

1. **Prompt.** `tools/art/texture_recipes.json` holds one subject line per surface and a shared template:
   a seamless swatch seen flat-on (top-down for floors and roofs, straight-on elevation for walls), evenly
   lit, with no vignette, objects, border or text, and flat cel tones with ink lines. The style preamble is
   added by `generate.sh` as usual, and every call is in `art/generation_log.jsonl`. Sources are in
   `art/generated/textures/<theme>_<surface>.png`.
2. **Seamless** (`blender/make_texture.py`, numpy inside Blender's Python because this machine has no PIL).
   A 768 px tile plus 192 px of overlap is taken from the 1024 px swatch. The overlap that runs past the right
   edge is pasted over the left edge and joined to the original along a **minimum-error cut** (dynamic
   programming, as in image quilting), then the same is done top to bottom. Line art is cut, never
   cross-faded, so there are no ghosted double lines. Each cut closes on itself (start and end within a
   pixel), so the other axis still wraps. The wainscot wraps in x only.
3. **Downscale** 768 to 512 px by exact area averaging on the tile's own grid, which keeps it seamless.
4. **Flatten light.** Luminance is divided by a wrap-around Gaussian blur of itself (gain clamped to
   0.8–1.25), so a vignette in the swatch doesn't repeat as a blotch.
5. **Palette.** The tile is snapped to the full palette, then to the colours that cover it (at most 8, each at
   least 0.6 %) or to a hand-picked list in the recipe (grass, roofs, planks, church flags, dungeon wall).
   Snapping to that subset removes stray hue flecks. `saturate` in a recipe boosts chroma first, because
   Gemini's muted greens and reds otherwise snap to grey and brown. A wrap-around despeckle runs last.

Rebuild everything with `make textures`, or one surface with `make textures ONLY="village/grass"`. Add
`GENERATE=1` to ask Gemini for new swatches first. The import settings come from `tools/art/set_import.py`
(VRAM BPTC with mipmaps). Those `.import` files are kept in git, like the walk sheets (`.gitignore`
exceptions).

The scales were checked in Godot at the combat camera's distance (see Preview). Smaller tiles, such as
real-size bricks or planks, turn to mush under mipmapping and the palette pass, so the details are drawn a
little larger than life.

## The Modern look's smooth tiles (`<surface>_hd.png`)
`make textures HD=1` (`blender/make_texture.py --hd`) writes, beside each palette-snapped tile, a 768 px tile from
the same swatch for the Modern finish (docs/art/style_bible.md): the snapped tile's colours softened so their flat
bands blend, with the swatch's own fine shading laid back over them, at the snapped tile's brightness. Its path is
the entry's `hd_file` in the manifest; `Look.cel_textured` uses it when the look is Modern, else the snapped tile.
No new images are generated for it. The snapped tiles are not touched.

## Applying them (for the lead)

- **Material.** `shaders/cel.gdshader` already has `albedo_tex` and `use_texture`. It *multiplies* `albedo`,
  so set `albedo` to `Color(1, 1, 1)` when a texture is on. That is a multiplier, not a drawn colour; the
  texture supplies the palette colours. Keep the cel `light()` and the palette pass: the pass re-snaps after
  lighting, so the tiles stay on the palette in the lit game.
- **Filtering.** Use mipmaps. The shader's sampler is `filter_nearest` with no mipmaps, so 512 px tiles
  spanning 1–4 units shimmer and moiré at the combat camera. Use `filter_linear_mipmap_anisotropic` for
  floors and roofs and `filter_linear_mipmap` for walls, with `repeat_enable`. The palette pass and its 2x
  pixel grid restore the crisp look, so nearest filtering buys nothing here.
- **UVs and tiling scale.** `BoxMesh` UVs are a 3 x 2 atlas, so `UV * uv_scale` won't tile cleanly. Map in
  world space from `world_pos` (already a varying in the cel shader) and the world-space normal, picking the
  dominant axis:
  - top and bottom faces: `uv = world_pos.xz`
  - faces whose normal points along x: `uv = vec2(world_pos.z, -world_pos.y)`
  - faces whose normal points along z: `uv = vec2(world_pos.x, -world_pos.y)`

  Then `uv /= tile_world_units`. The minus on y keeps the tile's top up on walls. World mapping keeps texel
  density equal on boxes of any size and lines up across neighbouring cells, so a floor of 1 x 1 boxes reads
  as one surface. `tools/art/preview/world_uv_cel.gdshader` is a working version (the cel lighting plus this
  mapping) to port into `shaders/cel.gdshader`.
- **Wainscot.** u as above with `tile_world_units` 1.2. On side faces v runs from 0 at the top of the wall to 1
  at the floor, `v = (wall_top - world_pos.y) / wall_height`, once over the wall height (1.15 units for
  cut-away interior walls), never repeated. The reference shader's `wall_band` option does this.
- **Village houses.** `house_wall` spans 2 units, so one copy covers the 2.0-unit house box. Put
  `roof_thatch` (poor houses) or `roof_slate` (the Burgomaster's, the church) on the roof box.
- **Repetition.** If a large floor shows the tile, offset the world UV per region (a hash of the room id,
  never of a dice roll), or mix `floor_alt` the way `ArenaBoard` already mixes two floor materials.

## Billboard props (`art/sprites/props/`)

Twelve single-view props: dead_tree, pine, lantern_post, gravestone, barrel, crate, bookshelf, bed, table,
well, market_stall, wagon. Each is generated on flat white and cut out with `make prop SRC=… ID=… HEIGHT=…`
(`blender/prop_sprite.py`). That removes the background, including gaps such as between wagon spokes, drops
specks, crops tight, scales the longer side to 512 px, makes alpha binary, palette-snaps, despeckles and pads
each side to a multiple of 4 (for VRAM compression). Imports are VRAM BPTC with mipmaps.

`art/sprites/props/manifest.json` gives each prop's file, pixel size, intended `world_height` in units, and
the `pixel_size` that produces that height. The bottom opaque row is the ground contact (anchor bottom
centre, 2 px margin). To apply in Godot:

- `Sprite3D` with `billboard = BaseMaterial3D.BILLBOARD_FIXED_Y` (props stay upright while facing the camera),
  `pixel_size` from the manifest, `centered = true` and `offset.y = texture height / 2`, so the node's
  origin is the prop's foot.
- `alpha_cut = SpriteBase3D.ALPHA_CUT_DISCARD` (alpha is already binary, so props sort and shade like
  geometry), `texture_filter = TEXTURE_FILTER_LINEAR_WITH_MIPMAPS`.
- Props are scenery, so they go through the palette pass like the boxes (unlike character sprites).
- Heights: dead tree 4.0, pine 5.0, lantern post 2.4, gravestone 0.9, barrel 0.8, crate 0.7, bookshelf 1.6,
  bed 0.8, table 0.7, well 1.8, market stall 2.0, wagon 1.8 units. Bed, table, crate and wagon are drawn in
  three-quarter view, so they read best seen from the front (the combat camera's usual angle).
- The lantern post's glow is painted on. Add a `CandleFlicker` light at its top, as `ArenaBoard._lanterns`
  does.

## Preview

`tools/art/preview/texture_preview.tscn` (not part of the game) lays each theme out on 5 ft boxes with the
reference shader, moonlight, the bruise ambient and the palette pass, and places the props. It takes one shot
per theme:

    make capture SCENE=res://tools/art/preview/texture_preview.tscn NAME=p4_09_textures FRAMES=30

This writes `captures/p4_09_textures_{village,vallaki,tavern,church,dungeon}.png`.
