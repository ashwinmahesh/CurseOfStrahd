# Set dressing: props, doors, houses (world-assets pass)

Date: 2026-10-06 · Owner request: "in the opening level, we are using brown boxes for everything. Let's use real
assets." Every prop, container, door, exit and town building in the locations built so far now has art. All of it
is generated by Gemini (`gemini-3.1-flash-image`) through `tools/art/generate.sh` (every call is in
`art/generation_log.jsonl`); nothing was downloaded.

## What changed on screen

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| A door was a 0.9 x 1.7 brown box | A frame in the wall line (posts and a lintel in the wall's material) and a leaf the size of the opening: oak, double, carved, iron gate, portcullis, crypt gate, curtain, bookcase. Opening it hides the leaf; the frame stays. A secret door looks like the wall around it until found. |
| A container was a brown box (darker when looted) | A billboard of what it is (chest, strongbox, wardrobe, trunk, footlocker, sideboard, broken cart ...), dimmer when emptied |
| A prop was a small coloured box; on a wall square it was hidden inside the wall | Standing props are billboards; wall things (portraits, fireplaces, windows, tapestries, trophies, robes on pegs) hang flat on the wall face that looks into the room; floor things (the wolf rug, stairwells, graves, rune circles, scorch marks) lie flat |
| '=' squares indoors were random boxes or beds | Furniture by room: armchairs, tables with chairs, settees, candelabra in manors; pews in churches; a bar, counters and shelves where '=' squares run in a line |
| '~' squares were black thorn cones | Brambles in the woods, rubble underground and indoors; a town's mud is its texture |
| Town houses were one 2 x 2-unit box per wall square under a flat slab | Whole houses (TownBuilder): plaster-and-timber or clapboard walls, gabled thatch or slate roofs, chimneys, shuttered windows (some lit), doors where the exits are; yard walls are low stone walls |
| Raised floors and water were checkerboards | Stone flags or boards; a water texture |
| Fires were orange cones | A flame billboard that sways; torches on poles |
| Exits had nothing | A door on a house front, a door in an interior's outer wall, stairs up or down |

Opening road specifics: the Gates of Barovia are two stone pillars with headless knight statues and an iron gate; the
mist the party came out of is a fog bank at the east edge; the first cottage stands at the village edge; the

## Pieces keep their place and facing (owner request, 2026-10-06)

"Environmental items shouldn't rotate with the view of the camera, we should see the correct view." Every piece now has
a fixed place and facing, and the camera sees the side that faces it:

- **Furniture against a wall** (bookcases, shelves, wardrobes, cabinets, sideboards, desks, counters, the bar, the
  organ, cask racks, clocks): its *front view* (catalog `fronts`, drawn straight on) stands flat and parallel to the
  wall beside it, a little way out, with a wooden body behind it back to the wall, so from the side it has thickness
  and from behind you see its back. On a wall square it hangs on the wall like a portrait.
- **Free-standing pieces with a front and a back** (chests, tables, beds, chairs, carts, wagons, statues, pews,
  signposts, stalls, the cottage and tent ...): drawn twice in one Gemini call, from the front and from behind, each
  with that side toward the lower right (`<id>` and `<id>_back`, linked by `back` in the manifest). `PropView`
  (`addons/dnd_core/world/look/prop_view.gd`) shows the front when the camera is in front of the piece and the back when it's
  behind, mirrored for the other corner. A piece faces away from a wall beside it, else south (toward the opening
  camera).
- **Pieces that look the same from every side** (barrels, wells, braziers, trees, boulders, brambles, towers) stay
  plain billboards: they show the right view from anywhere.
- `tools/art/preview/prop_turntable.tscn` shoots a small room of these from the four camera headings
  (`make capture SCENE=res://tools/art/preview/prop_turntable.tscn NAME=turntable`).

## Stairs are steps (owner report, 2026-10-06)

"Stairs still look too small... the icon for stairs down looks weird." Stairs are no longer pictures: `Stairs`
(`addons/dnd_core/world/look/stairs.gd`) builds a flight up as seven solid steps climbing 7.5 ft across its square, with a banister,
and a stairwell down as a shaft that opens the square's floor (stone sides, a dark bottom) with steps descending 7 ft.
Both start at the square's open side and climb or descend away from it, in wood indoors and stone below ground, and
read right from every camera heading. Exits and props whose art is `stair_riser` or `stair_down` use them.

## Flush with the wall, always

Furniture that has a front view is never drawn as a turning billboard: it stands flush with the wall or doorway
beside it (the Death House's swinging bookcase backs onto its secret door), or with its back to the north when
nothing is beside it, and its root stands on its own square so it shows and hides with that square.
`test_wall_pieces_sit_flush_with_their_wall` fails on any wall piece turned off its wall or without a wall behind
it, and on any front-view furniture drawn as a billboard.

## Pointing at pieces (owner report, 2026-10-06)

The top of a tall piece is drawn over the squares behind it, so the mouse now tests what's drawn before the floor
(`LocationView.pick_cell`, `addons/dnd_core/world/exploration/sprite_pick.gd`): each person's and piece's quad as drawn, nearest the
camera first, and for pieces the texture's alpha, so only painted pixels count. Floor pieces and empty floor fall
back to the floor square (`GridPick`).

## Rooms and places have their own surfaces (owner request, 2026-10-06)

"We want a variety of textures and assets to really capture the visual feel." The board picks each room's floor and
walls, most specific first:

1. An area's own `floor` and `walls` in the location data (texture surfaces such as `interior/tile_floor`; optional,
   in `data/schemas/location.schema.json`).
2. The catalog's `rooms` rules, matched on words in the area's name or id: bathrooms are tiled with damp tiles on
   the walls, kitchens and bakeries have stone flags and sooty brick, libraries, studies, dens and dining rooms
   parquet and dark panelling, halls and landings marble, bedrooms and parlours carpet and green damask, nurseries
   bird wallpaper, chapels and naves stone, crypts and vaults dungeon stone, cells and barracks damp brick, the
   surgery whitewash and tiles, workshops sawdust boards.
3. The place's look (`place_looks`): the Amber Temple's black stone veined with amber, Mount Baratok's scree and snow
   inside cliffs, the Tsolenka Pass's snow, the werewolf caves' rock, Berez's marsh mud.
4. The map's theme, as before.

A wall square takes the style of the room on its open side. `rock_walls` draws a place's wall squares as craggy
columns of cliff instead of trees. Textures: 26 new sets, from `tools/art/texture_recipes.json` (`make textures`).

## Real-world sizes (owner report, 2026-10-06)

"There is a small hut in the opening area... ensure we are sizing resources according to how big they should be."
The cottage had been shrunk to fit one square among the trees. Every standing piece now has the height of its picture
at real size in catalog `feet` (a person is 6 ft and one unit is 5 ft; low, deep things such as beds and tables count
the top seen from above): a wardrobe 7 ft, a table 3.5, a bed 4, a cottage 16, a gallows 15. A piece wider than
`big_width` (1.6 units) at that size, such as a cottage, tent, wagon or the standing stones, keeps its size whatever
the room and clears the trees (and the board's own stumps and brambles) on the squares it covers, plus one more row
on the west and south, the sides the opening camera looks from, so no tree stands in front of it. A narrower piece
with `big` in its catalog entry keeps its size the same way (the horses, two squares long, would shrink to ponies).
Furniture against a wall is squeezed sideways to its square but keeps its height.
`test_pieces_are_drawn_at_their_real_size` builds every location and fails on any piece drawn under 70% or over 135%
of its real size.

## Pieces don't overlap (owner report, 2026-10-06)

Every square a location's things stand on is reserved before the board is dressed (`SetDressing.reserve`). A standing
piece is no wider than its square unless all eight squares around it are open floor with nothing on them; the
board's own furniture picks a choice that fits (a wagon only where a wagon has room); furniture against a wall is
one square wide when the next square along the wall has something; and one wall face holds one piece (a second moves
to another open face; a piece of furniture against a wall claims the face behind it). A wall piece hangs on the side
facing its own area first. `test_no_piece_overlaps_another_or_a_wall` builds every location and fails on any overlap.

## How a thing finds its art

`art/sprites/props/catalog.json`, read by `addons/dnd_core/world/look/set_dressing.gd` (`SetDressing`):

1. `ids[<prop id>]`, then `models[<model>]`, then art with the model's own name, then `container_default` for
   containers. A value is an art id, `{art, mount, scale, on_wall}`, or `null` for an invisible spot (something
   already drawn, such as a facade whose exit draws its door, or a view).
2. `mount` comes from the art's manifest entry (`art/sprites/props/manifest.json`): `stand`, `wall` or `floor`.
3. Doors: `doors.ids`, then the first `doors.rules` entry whose words are in the door's id or label, then
   `doors.default`. Exits: `exits.rules`, then `outdoor_default` / `indoor_default`.
4. `low_cover.<theme>` dresses '=' squares (with `<theme>_run` / `<theme>_wall_run` for runs of three or more, so
   a long table or a bar is one piece of furniture), `difficult.<theme>` dresses '~' squares. `low_cover.<place>`
   overrides it for one location (the Wizard of Wines' rows are vines).
5. `scales` sizes a piece wherever it stands (stairs fill their square, beds and coffins are full length); `floors`
   gives a place its own ground (grass on Bonegrinder's hill); `town_walls` makes a walled town's wall tall (Krezk).
6. `building: true` on an entry makes the piece stand in for the whole house on its square (Old Bonegrinder's
   windmill, the Abbey's bell tower, Van Richten's tower), standing whole and fading where it hides the party,
   as the houses do (`SeeThrough`, docs/art/building_kit.md); `see_through: true` does the same for a
   tall piece of stone or timber (the Amber Temple's colossus, Baba Lysaga's hut); `fade: true` fades a tall piece
   like the trees (the Gulthias Tree).

New content: give a new prop a `model` that names art, or add an entry. `tests/integration/test_set_dressing.gd`
fails when a location has a prop, container or door without art.

## Placement rules

- **Stand:** `Sprite3D`, `BILLBOARD_FIXED_Y`, origin at the foot, alpha cut, linear filtering with mipmaps, lit.
  Props are scenery, so they go through the palette pass (unlike characters). A stand prop on a wall square takes
  that square: the board hides its tree or wall block while the prop exists (`ArenaBoard.clear_cell`) and puts it
  back when the prop is freed. On a town house it hangs on the wall instead (its `on_wall` art if it has one).
- **Wall:** flat on the face of the wall square toward its open neighbour (faces the default camera sees first),
  0.012 in front, never wider than the face. Pieces 0.85 units or taller stand on the floor; smaller ones hang at
  about eye level. A piece on an open square hangs on the wall beside it. It fades with its wall where it hides
  the party (`SeeThrough`); on a house it goes with the house when the house is cut away, and hides the window it covers.
- **Floor:** flat (`axis = Y`), centred on the square.
- **Doors:** the leaf is centred in its square, turned to lie in the wall line, scaled to 0.86 of the square and the
  wall's height (1.15 indoors, the cut-away height; 1.9 outdoors). The Gates of Barovia (`doors.ids`) use
  `pillars` and a `statue`.

## Towns (`addons/dnd_core/world/look/town_builder.gd`)

Inner wall squares that belong to some 2 x 2 block of wall are houses; the rest are yard walls. Each solid block is
split into rectangles (largest first, growing right then down) and each rectangle becomes one house: walls 2.2
units tall (more for large houses), a gable along the longer side, thatch for small village houses and slate for big
ones (Vallaki is all slate), a stone chimney on most, and windows on every third open face. Since W7 the houses, yard
walls and Vallaki's palisade are put together from the building kit in each region's style (docs/art/building_kit.md). Roofs are flat-shaded meshes; their texture comes from world position (`cel_world.gdshader`), the same as
the boxes. A house standing between the camera and the party's leader stands whole and fades while it hides the party
(`SeeThrough`, owner 2026-10-17, 2026-10-19; it went see-through whole from 2026-10-13); with the catalog's
`building_kit.cut_away` on it would be cut away: its roof and wall pieces hide and its walls drop to 0.5 units
(`TownBuilder.cut_away`, called each frame with the tree fade).

## Making more art

`tools/art/prop_recipes.json` holds the prompts. A sheet is four objects in a 2 x 2 grid, one Gemini call, cut
apart by `blender/lib/propsheet.py` (each shape goes to the quarter its box centre is in; ruled lines Gemini
sometimes adds between the quarters are removed). Singles are for big things (the cottage, the tent, the collapsed
barn). The cleanup is the walk sheets' (`docs/art/p4_cast_art_pass.md`): bilateral smoothing, area downscale to
about 256 texels per world unit (512 max, never upscaled), palette snap, despeckle and small-clump merging.

    make props GENERATE=1 ONLY="sheet_name"     # new or replaced sheets (2 calls at a time, backoff on errors)
    make props                                  # re-cut every sheet from its source
    make import && python3 tools/art/set_import.py art/sprites/props/*.png && make import

This pass made 43 sheets (157 pieces) and one texture (`wild/water`) in 45 Gemini calls (one sheet was redrawn).

## Checking it

`tools/art/preview/location_tour.tscn` builds a location as in the game, then shoots an overview and close shots
around its doors, props and containers:

    make capture SCENE=res://tools/art/preview/location_tour.tscn LOCATION=death_house_ground NAME=tour ARGS="--lit --shots=6"

`--lit` adds a work light for checking placement in dark interiors; `--hour=` sets the time outdoors.

## Livelier outdoor places (lane 28, 2026-10-08)

"The Tser pool encampment looks really boring... more tents, carts, things to interact, more interesting scenery,
things to find, more people to talk to", and "the great tent... should be wayyy bigger based on its description".

- **Facing and span.** A prop's `facing` (north, south, east, west or between) turns its piece to look that way, and
  `span` ([across, down] squares, its own cell the north-west one) stands a piece bigger than its square over the middle
  of its block of low cover. Tser Pool's wagons ring the fire facing it, each over two squares by two.
- **Things to stand on** (Ashwin: "larger things that change elevation and can be interacted with"). A prop's
  `stand_ft` on a piece whose top sits at that height (a vardo's roof at 10 ft, a broken cart or a treading vat at 5, a
  dead tree climbed into at 10) makes its squares high ground; built digits make platforms and walks (Krezk's wall
  walk, the winery's watch platform), and `_` a low dais two feet up (Tser Pool's stage, Vallaki's podium, altars).
- **Big pieces clear what's under them.** A building-sized 3D piece hides the trees and the board's own furniture on
  every square its footprint covers, however big (it was two squares each way at most).
- **Fences run along their rails** (catalog `runs_along`): a pen's east and west rails go up the map. A place's
  `low_cover.<place>` makes its '=' squares fences (Tser Pool's horse pen) or tumbled field walls (the open road).
- **On the map.** Catalog `map_marks` draws building-sized pieces on the minimap over their squares: the great tent a
  striped round of its canvas colours, tents smaller rounds, wagons in their paint.
- **Madam Eva's great tent** is its own model (`great_tent`, blender/models_3d.py): patched canvas on two poles, seven
  squares by five, painted with suns, moons and eyes, glowing at its flap. Its 2D fallback is a render of the model.
  The camp's stew pot (`stewpot`) is a black pot of stew on a tripod over coals, not the witch's cauldron.

## Scale-ups (owner todos 2026-10-17)

"The dragon's desk and chair ... should be made bigger to represent the scale they are." The sizes follow the book's
dragon, an adult silver dragon, Huge (a 15 ft space), rearing 20 ft; the book gives no length, only "enormous" for the
skull (250 lb, Castle Ravenloft K67) and "taller than you are" for the tithe's silver (our Narrator line).

- **A prop's `scale`** (data/schemas/location.schema.json; props and containers) draws its 3D piece that many times its
  modelled size (ModelPiece.stand and hang take it; SetDressing.place marks the holder `scaled`). The squares round it
  that it covers are reserved (`SetDressing.scaled_squares`), so Furnish and clutter keep off them; test_models_3d's
  size checks allow the extra reach a `scale` asks for and no more. Nothing in the rules grid changes.
- **Argynvostholt's study:** the dragon's desk and chair are three times a desk's and a chair's size (a 15 ft desk, its
  top at 7.5 ft; a 19 ft chair back). The chair moved from [17, 3] to [17, 2] so it doesn't sit in the desk, and the
  hidden-drawer notes from [16, 4] to [17, 5], in front of the desk where they show.
- **The dragon's skull** (Castle Ravenloft's trophy room) is 2.2 times the model: 8.8 ft long, hung in profile among the
  stag and wolf heads (two stag heads moved from the north wall to the east wall to make room for it). **The
  mausoleum's bones** are 2.2 times: a 23 ft body lying on the bier with the wings drooped to the sides (28 ft across),
  its neck ending in nothing; the "head of the bier" prop moved to the neck's end at [3, 5]. Both are real models now
  (below), no longer pieces sculpted from the 2D sprites.
- **Sourced models** (owner picks, 2026-10-18): the skull is Paulina's "Dragon Skull" and the skeleton mgsawwinhtike3002's
  "Dragon skeleton low-poly", both CC BY 4.0 from Sketchfab, kept as downloaded in art/sourced/sketchfab_*/ (SOURCE.md,
  licence rows in docs/assets/LICENSES.md, credits in art/credits.json; `.gdignore` keeps Godot from importing them).
  `blender/models_3d.py` reads them (`make models ONLY="dragon_skull dragon_bones"`): the skull is thinned to 8.3k
  triangles and each face takes the nearest of four bone colours from the painted texture, smoothed over the mesh; the
  skeleton, a fixed pose with no rig, loses its head and the rock and base it stood on, lowers its neck, and each wing
  (the bones joined to a shoulder blade) is laid flat, drooped and shrunk to a quarter about its shoulder, then
  the whole is thinned to 10k triangles and settled to 0.6 of its height. The download has no tail, so the builder adds
  one (`_dragon_tail`: vertebrae from the pelvis, curling away from the hind legs along the dais's near edge). Their
  `size` in art/models/manifest.json is what the data's `scale` multiplies.
- **Silver heaps** (`silver_heap`, `addons/dnd_core/world/look/coin_heap.gd`): the Undercroft's eight heaps of silver are built from
  about a thousand coins each over a lumpy dune. The gold heap model scaled up was a smooth blob with a few big discs
  on it. Pillars and fight squares keep clear of them; the biggest, at [19, 5], is 7.5 ft tall.
- **Sprites** are sized by `CombatToken.HEIGHTS`: the Amber Sentinel stands 6.0 units (30 ft, the amber colossus that
  steps down off its plinth, Ashwin's pick in the Amber Temple plan; was 11.5 ft) and Argynvost's echo 4.0 (20 ft, was
  8.5 ft). This sizes the overworld figure only: a fight draws the Amber Golem block (Large, 2 x 2 squares, drawn at 2.0,
  inside test_creature_sizes' Large band of 1.3 to 2.8), which keeps its squares, reach and win rate. The Sentinel's
  Narrator line (sentinel.dialogue, its arrival) says "thirty feet tall" now (it said "three times a man's height",
  about 18 ft; Amber Temple step 8).
- **The beholder** (the Eye Below, a Large aberration in the 2025 Monster Manual) is drawn 2.5 units (12.5 ft across with
  its stalks, was 1.9): the same `CombatToken.HEIGHTS` entry for its own sprite, in a fight and out of one, near the
  top of the Large band (an ogre is 2.1, a giant elk 2.3) so its body fills its 2 x 2 squares. The Dream-Gazer, drawn
  with the same sprite, keeps its own `art_height` (0.55).

## Not right yet

- A long piece (a bar, a pew) drawn on each square of a run reads as a row of pieces rather than one long one. (Tables are
  one long piece now: "Things placed by hand" below; bars and counters are flush end to end already, pews are lined up
  on purpose.)
- A piece's back is Gemini's idea of its back, drawn alongside the front; a few pairs differ in colour.
- Wall pieces and door leaves are flat: seen along the wall they disappear, as the cut-away walls' faces do.
- Houses bend (lean, bow, sag; docs/art/building_kit.md "Natural shapes") but their footprints are the data's rectangles: an L-shaped block is one building under one joined roof, its wings' roofs meeting in a valley.
- Interiors stay dark at dusk and night by design; the party's lantern lights the pieces near the party.

## Things placed by hand (Natural Maps Plan I3, the catalog's `hand_placed`; owner 2026-10-19)

"We don't need to draw our real world maps as a battle grid": indoors, in the Modern look, pieces people set down are
not set square on their square's middle.

- **Loose pieces** (`hand_placed.loose`: a barrel, a crate, a basket, a sack, a chair, a stool, a stack of books, a pot, a
  bale, a small table ...): each stands between half of `shift` and all of it (`shift` is a fifth of a square) off its
  square's middle and is turned between half of `turn` and all of it (`turn` is 15 degrees) from the way it faced (never
  less than half, so none stands nearly square), seeded by its square (`ModelPiece.hand_offsets`), so a place is built the
  same way every time. `ModelPiece.hand_place` (3D pieces, called by `ModelPiece.stand`) never lets the turned piece leave
  its square, never moves it toward a wall or door beside it (what hangs on the wall, and the wall's face, stay clear),
  and leaves a piece alone when the location's data puts another prop or container on its square (a pot on a hearth);
  `ModelPiece.hand_shift` does the same for a 2D piece (`SetDressing.stand_piece`). A prop with its own `facing`, or
  spanning squares, keeps it. Outdoors (the wilds' own passes), the Classic look and pieces not listed are untouched.
- **Pieces people line up** (pews, beds, shelves, wardrobes, counters, a table against a wall) are not in the list and keep
  their lines.
- **A long run of tables** (`hand_placed.one_piece`): a straight run of three or more `=` squares of one of these, with
  nothing of the location's on it, is built as long tables (`ArenaBoard._one_piece`, from `_low_cover`): the model's
  middle stretched and its ends as modelled (`ModelPiece.instance_long`, so the legs stand at the ends), up to five
  squares a table, a table of twelve being three on their own legs. The squares are still low cover to the rules.
  Counters and bars are flush end to end already and stay a row.

## Mount Baratok's treeline (the vault's Mount Baratok Look Plan, 2026-10-19)

`rock_walls` draws every `#` of a place as a rock column, which on Mount Baratok was a field of grey cubes. Beside
`rock_walls` a place look may carry `tree_walls`, rectangles `[[x0, y0], [x1, y1]]` (corners included) of wall squares
that are drawn as the place's trees all the same (`ArenaBoard._tree_wall`, asked by `_wall`): in the Modern look only,
so the frozen Classic look keeps its rock. The rules are untouched: they are still walls. Baratok's lower slopes
(the south approach and its pillars, the drop under the lookout, the field's two inner blocks, the east edge) are the
snow-laden spruce of its plant set and the crags above (rows 0 to 8 and the north-east band) stay bare rock. A prop
standing on a wall square hides its rock or tree as before; a prop of `art: pine` stays the old low-poly cone, since
props are built after the land's snowy trees, so a place's woods come from `tree_walls`, not from props.

A catalog entry for a prop id (`ids`) may also carry `swap`: a 3D piece's surfaces drawn in other materials, by the names
Blender gave them (`ModelPiece.swap_surfaces`, called by `SetDressing._place` beside `tint`, before the piece opens
round the party). Mount Baratok's hut is the half-timbered thatched cottage with `tex_village__house_wall` swapped for
`tex_dungeon__stone_wall` (the hut interior's own stone), the thatch for `tex_village__roof_slate` and its timbers for
dark stone: honestly a stone cottage with a slate roof. A rough-stone hut of its own shape needs a Blender model.

## Cliffs and drops with shape (the vault's Natural Maps Plan, N6)

Where natural ground drops 15 ft or more between two squares (`CombatGrid.is_cliff`), or over an empty square
(`ArenaBoard._over_a_drop`: a lake, a gorge, a fall), the Modern look no longer draws a straight vertical slab of rock on
a flat step: `CliffFace` (planned once by `ArenaBoard`, kept per place) draws the squares round each cliff as one height
field, in a grid of 6 points a square. Each cliff side is a unit edge with a high side and a low side; the ground steps
from one level to the other through a face whose line wanders in and out of the grid line by no more than a quarter of a
square (the plan's rule 2; broad lobes plus flutes), rounds off at a plateau's corner (a smooth minimum of the distances),
has a shelf half way down where the noise says, and spreads at its foot into a talus under a low apron of scree
(0.25 high, gone by half a square) with a few boulders along it. Over a drop the lip reaches out over the empty square by a
tenth to half a square. Where the surface is steep (or the apron) it is the place's rock (`ArenaBoard._cliff_material`), else
its ground. The squares touching a cliff side (`CliffFace.draws`) leave their column's top and sides out; the high square's
column gets two children, `Top` (its ground, in its own floor material) and `Cliff` (the rock hung from its sides, even where
it reaches over the low square), so they hide with the floor as the slab did. A square's middle stays where a token stands
(`ground_y`), and 0.55 of a square from a cliff the field is the board's own ground. Classic keeps the slabs.
Cosmetic only; `tools/capture/crags_views.tscn` (CV_NOCLIFFS=1 shoots a place as it was) and test_cliffs check it.

## Crags, not columns (owner 2026-10-19: "why are all of our mountain tops flat?")

A place look with `rock_walls` may also say `rock_shape: "crags"` (Modern only; Classic keeps the columns). Its rock
squares are then one shaped mountain (`Crags`, planned by `ArenaBoard._plan_crags` before any square is built): the
surface rises out of the walked ground in a ragged face, climbs the deeper the rock runs (past the map's edge counts
as more rock) and breaks into peaks, ridges and saddles from noise seeded by the place. The face's foot wanders from a
quarter of a square out over the walkable squares beside the rock to two-thirds of a square in under it (the plan's
rule 2: edges bend both ways, and a face is taller than a knee, so a quarter at most), and a low scree apron of the same
rock skirts it, gone by half a square from the grid line, so a token in the middle of the square beside the rock has
its ring clear and the ground at its feet. Each rock square keeps its own piece of the surface (`Crags.piece`, meta
`crag`; the quads of the walkable squares the foot and scree reach into go with the nearest rock square's piece), so
it fades in the party's way (from farther off when it's tall: meta `fade_reach`) and a prop can still take its square.
GroundRelief draws a crag's square flat in the place's own ground rather than as a woods bank, so the foot rises out
of the same ground the party stands on. Two more place look keys tune it: `crag_floor` (world units: no point more
than half a square in from open ground stands lower, so a thin band of rock stays a ridge instead of breaking into
lone spikes; its crest stands at 0.55 to 1.2 times that, peaks and saddles meandering along the band; the Amber Temple
road, Tsolenka, the Ghakis quarry and the Amber road camp) and `crag_scale` (a multiplier on the heights; the castle gates, so
the castle stays the tallest thing). Crags are on Mount Baratok, its trail, the Tsolenka Pass, the werewolf den, the
castle gates, the Amber Temple road, and the two shrine-yard maps in rock country, the Ghakis quarry and the Amber
road camp (their yard walls, pillars and corner blocks are rock, not stone, and TownBuilder builds no sheds there: their
look says `no_sheds`, `ArenaBoard._no_sheds`; Classic keeps them; the castle's overlook has `rock_walls` but its builder claims every wall
square, and most of the gates' and the pass's walls are buildings too, so only their loose rock is shaped).
Boulders lie along every foot (`Crags._scatter_boulders`, drawn with the rock square's piece): about two a side of a rock
square that a walkable square touches, low faceted rocks no taller than a knee (0.32) that reach no further than half
a square from the grid line, so the middle of the square stays clear (the plan's rule 2: "scree and boulders up to half
a square").
The range carries on past the map's edge (the vault's Natural Maps Plan, N8): where a rock square stands on the edge,
`AtmosphereLand._terrain` raises the land past it by the rock's height there (`Crags.beyond_edge`: the edge profile,
blurred a square either way so it ramps down beside a way out, settling into the land over 8 squares and ridged by
noise), draws the risen land in the place's rock (`ArenaBoard.rock_material`, a fourth surface of the land mesh) and
plants no tree or ground plant on it, so a turned or tilted camera sees the mountain go on instead of a wall and a forest.
tools/capture/crags_views.tscn shoots them from the four headings and prints which squares a crag hides.
## Ground cover and small built things as natural land (owner 2026-10-19; the vault's Natural Maps Plan, N7)

"Basically we want the world to look more natural": draw each place as natural land first, lay the battle grid over it
afterwards. Four small things that were one box or bush a square are now drawn across their squares, in the Modern look
only (Classic keeps what it had); the rules grid never sees any of it.

- **Thickets** (`GroundCover`, asked by `ArenaBoard._brambles`). A map's difficult squares are planted together, once: clumps of
  the place's own dressing (catalog `difficult`: brambles, reeds, drifts, ice) scattered by noise over each run of `~`,
  big where the run is deep and small at its rim, thick in some patches and thin in others, turned along the run's edge
  and stretched their own way (drifts long and low), standing wherever the noise puts them, across the squares' edges.
  A clump may reach a third of a square into the open ground beside the run and never into water or the void, and every
  difficult square keeps a clump near its middle so it reads as difficult. Clumps are `ground_cover`, so they may grow
  into each other; each belongs to the difficult square its middle is in, so a prop that takes the square takes them.
  Outdoors only: a place's own indoor set (rubble, webs) stays one piece a square until the interiors package.
- **Standing stones** (`StandingStone`, asked by `ArenaBoard._wall`'s shrine-yard branch). An inner `#` of a yard that is
  no part of a building is a weathered stone of its own (`Pillar`, meta `standing_stone`): a ring grid tapering as it
  climbs, leaning, its sides wobbling, its top rounded, cut slantwise, split into two horns or ragged; a free-standing 2 x 2
  is one bigger tor. Cave rock by default (place look `stone` names another surface). Narrower than the square, so the
  ground is drawn under it; never reaching out of its square.
- **Dry stone** (`Drystone`, asked by `_wall` and `_low_cover`). The yard's outer wall and its low walls are laid as coping
  stones, two to a square, each a hair off a swell that carries on across the squares, with a bevel and a top a little
  askew; here and there one is broken (lower, slanting) or missing, but none is ever lower than half the whole wall. The
  outer wall stands on a rough footing the width of its square, its body set back a changing few inches; corners are a post
  a little taller than either arm. Low walls run from the middle of their square toward each low neighbour and join where
  they meet. Meta `drystone` is "outer", "low" or "yard".
- **Ruin** (`Ruin`, place look `ruin`: true, or 0 to 1 for how far). Argynvostholt, Berez and the Gulthias Tree's yard
  are ruins: their walls are drawn broken, to a height no lower than three fifths of the whole wall at the most
  fallen, with more stones broken and missing and the whole a little lower. Yard walls and low walls are `Drystone`
  there (the kit's whole yard wall, and the `wall_low` pieces Berez set on its `=` squares, are replaced); houses stand
  as they were. Geometry, not a shader that clips the top: a clipped top is hollow and shows the ground through it.

`Drystone.enabled`, `StandingStone.enabled` and `GroundCover.enabled` switch them off for tests and timing runs that
compare with the old pieces. tests/integration/test_natural_small_things.gd.

## Indoor rubble, water and webs that blend (the vault's Natural Maps Plan, I4)

Every `~` square of an interior or a dungeon carried a material of its own and one piece of dressing, so a run of rubble, a
flooded passage or a hall of webs was a rectangle of square tiles with a cube or a web in each. In the Modern look two
things change, on every board whose floors are boxes (interiors, dungeons, an outdoor dungeon map, a board with no place;
N2's skin draws the rest):

- **A patch that blends** (`FloorPatch`, laid by `ArenaBoard._build` once the floors are built). The run's squares and the
  squares round it are covered by one thin flat patch, a child of each square's floor box (so a stairwell or a secret room
  that hides the floor hides the patch), drawn with N2's blend (`Look.blend_ground`): the difficult surface and the
  room's own floor (the three most used round the run) melt into each other at the run's edge, which the shader breaks up
  with noise and lets spill up to half a square into the floor beside it; a difficult square's middle 60% is always its
  own surface. The difficult surface is the place look's `difficult`, else the theme's (a dungeon's mud), else packed
  earth. The floor boxes under it and beyond it are untouched, so the room's tiles are kept.
- **Clumps across squares** (`GroundCover`, as outdoors): rubble heaps and webs scattered by noise over the run instead of
  one on each square. Walls here are built, so a clump keeps out of them; rubble (a heap, `GroundCover.HEAPS`) is turned any
  way and not stretched long, and a sprite stays under a token's height.

`FloorPatch.enabled` switches the patch off for tests and timing runs; tests/integration/test_natural_small_things.gd.
## Caves, not rooms (owner 2026-10-19: "Ex cave rooms should have natural shapes instead of it all being square rooms")

A cave's rock was drawn like a house's walls, a box a square a storey high with a flat black cap, so a great cave was a
rectangle cut out of a slab. A place look with `cave: true` (or `cave: "hewn"`, the lighter amount, for rooms cut into
the rock) draws it as `Caves` instead, in the Modern look only (Classic keeps its blocks; the vault's "Natural Maps
Plan", I2). `InteriorWalls.build` asks `Caves.of(board)` for each wall square and lets the cave draw the squares it
claims; the cave is planned the first time it's asked, once for the whole board (`Caves._plan`), and kept for the next
visit like GroundRelief's arrays (the newest eight places).

- **The outline.** The floor's boundary is traced into loops and every corner rounded as far as the walls beside it
  allow: a corner between two long walls takes a tangent of up to two squares (the curve's control point is pushed out
  into the rock, as far as that rock is thick, so the cut over the floor stays a fifth of a square), a one-square niche
  hardly rounds. The loops are filled into texels, a signed distance (a chamfer sweep) and a small blur make the field,
  and noise seeded by the place moves the contour: a slow winding, a wobble, and bays that swell into the rock by up to
  what its thickness leaves (a wall between two rooms keeps a square of rock, a one-square wall seven tenths, and nothing swells through the map's own edge), never more than a
  quarter of a square in over the floor (rule 2 of the plan: a token in a square's middle and its ring stay clear;
  `test_caves` measures it). The grid is then moved onto the field's levels near the contour (a third of a square apart)
  so the rows of the surface run along the wall, not along the grid.
- **The rock.** One surface over that field, each rock square keeping a piece of its own (meta `cave`): a steep face
  that leans over the cave toward its top, a ragged lip, a dark top (the cave rock's `CUT_FACE` colour, as every
  interior's wall top), and at its foot a slope of rubble that runs up to half a square out over the floor, with loose
  stones and, in the bays the walls swell into, stalagmites. The rock far from any cave (more than two squares) is one
  plain top, in chunks three squares across (`CaveRockFar`, narrower than SeeThrough's `LARGE`, so a chunk fades where a line of sight crosses its box, not the whole map's). Everything fades where it hides the party like any wall (SeeThrough). Bays get a floor box of the
  room's own floor; the floor carries loose stones, and puddles where the look says `cave_puddles` (the den).
- **What stays straight.** A wall square with something on it (a prop, a door's gap, an exit; anything in
  `ArenaBoard.occupied` on a wall) keeps a straight, plumb stretch of wall about two squares long, so what hangs on it
  hangs on a wall. A look's `built_areas` name areas that stay built (Ghakis's keepers' post): a wall that faces only
  those, and a free-standing stack of shelves (the drowned library's), are InteriorWalls' as before, and a door in a gap
  of the rock brings its own frame.
- **Keys.** `cave` (true or "hewn"), `built_areas` (area ids), `cave_height` (the rock's top above the floor, default
  2.6), `cave_puddles`. The amounts are `Caves.AMOUNTS`. `docs/art/interiors.md` lists the places.
- **Looking at it.** `make capture SCENE=res://tools/capture/caves_capture.tscn NAME=caves/x` shoots the cave places
  whole, from straight above and close from each heading with the screens hidden (`CV_LIGHT=1` adds a strong lamp, so
  the shape can be read in a map made to be dark; `CV_OFF=1` the old blocks; `CV_FORCE=amber_deep` the look on a place
  that doesn't have it yet; `CV_TIME=n` builds each place n times with it off and on for the build times).
