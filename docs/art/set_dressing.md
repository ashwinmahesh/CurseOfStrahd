# Set dressing: props, doors, houses (world-assets pass)

Date: 2026-10-06 · Owner request: "in the opening level, we are using brown boxes for everything. Let's use real
assets." Every prop, container, door, exit and town building in the locations built so far now has art. All of it
is generated by Gemini (`gemini-3.1-flash-image`) through `tools/art/generate.sh` (every call is in
`art/generation_log.jsonl`); nothing was downloaded.

## What changed on screen

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| A door was a 0.9 x 1.7 brown box | A frame in the wall line (posts and a lintel in the wall's material) and a leaf the size of the opening: oak, double, carved, iron gate, portcullis, crypt gate, curtain, bookcase. Opening it hides the leaf; the frame stays. A secret door looks like the wall around it until found. |
| A container was a brown box (darker when looted) | A billboard of what it is (chest, strongbox, wardrobe, trunk, footlocker, sideboard, broken cart ...), dimmer when emptied |
| A prop was a small coloured box; on a wall square it was hidden inside the wall | Standing props are billboards; wall things (portraits, fireplaces, windows, tapestries, trophies, robes on pegs) hang flat on the wall face that looks into the room; floor things (the wolf rug, stairwells, graves, rune circles, scorch marks) lie flat |
| '=' squares indoors were random boxes or beds | Furniture by room: armchairs, tables with chairs, settees, candelabra in manors; pews in churches; a bar, counters and shelves where '=' squares run in a line |
| '~' squares were black thorn cones | Brambles in the woods, rubble underground and indoors; a town's mud is its texture |
| Town houses were one 2 x 2-unit box per wall square under a flat slab | Whole houses (TownBuilder): plaster-and-timber or clapboard walls, gabled thatch or slate roofs, chimneys, shuttered windows (some lit), doors where the exits are; yard walls are low stone walls |
| Raised floors and water were checkerboards | Stone flags or boards; a water texture |
| Fires were orange cones | A flame billboard that sways; torches on poles |
| Exits had nothing | A door on a house front, a door in an interior's outer wall, stairs up or down |

Opening road specifics: the Gates of Barovia are two stone pillars with headless knight statues and an iron gate; the
mist the party came out of is a fog bank at the east edge; the first cottage stands at the village edge; the
signpost, wayside shrine, deer carcass, statue heads, crow and abandoned cart are where the data puts them.

## How a thing finds its art

`art/sprites/props/catalog.json`, read by `world/look/set_dressing.gd` (`SetDressing`):

1. `ids[<prop id>]`, then `models[<model>]`, then art with the model's own name, then `container_default` for
   containers. A value is an art id, `{art, mount, scale, on_wall}`, or `null` for an invisible spot (something
   already drawn, such as a facade whose exit draws its door, or a view).
2. `mount` comes from the art's manifest entry (`art/sprites/props/manifest.json`): `stand`, `wall` or `floor`.
3. Doors: `doors.ids`, then the first `doors.rules` entry whose words are in the door's id or label, then
   `doors.default`. Exits: `exits.rules`, then `outdoor_default` / `indoor_default`.
4. `low_cover.<theme>` dresses '=' squares (with `<theme>_run` / `<theme>_wall_run` for runs of three or more, so
   a long table or a bar is one piece of furniture), `difficult.<theme>` dresses '~' squares.

New content: give a new prop a `model` that names art, or add an entry. `tests/integration/test_set_dressing.gd`
fails when a location has a prop, container or door without art.

## Placement rules

- **Stand:** `Sprite3D`, `BILLBOARD_FIXED_Y`, origin at the foot, alpha cut, linear filtering with mipmaps, lit.
  Props are scenery, so they go through the palette pass (unlike characters). A stand prop on a wall square takes
  that square: the board hides its tree or wall block while the prop exists (`ArenaBoard.clear_cell`) and puts it
  back when the prop is freed. On a town house it hangs on the wall instead (its `on_wall` art if it has one).
- **Wall:** flat on the face of the wall square toward its open neighbour (faces the default camera sees first),
  0.012 in front, never wider than the face. Pieces 0.85 units or taller stand on the floor; smaller ones hang at
  about eye level. A piece on an open square hangs on the wall beside it. On a house it goes with the house when the
  house is cut away, and hides the window it covers.
- **Floor:** flat (`axis = Y`), centred on the square.
- **Doors:** the leaf is centred in its square, turned to lie in the wall line, scaled to 0.86 of the square and the
  wall's height (1.15 indoors, the cut-away height; 1.9 outdoors). The Gates of Barovia (`doors.ids`) use
  `pillars` and a `statue`.

## Towns (`world/look/town_builder.gd`)

Inner wall squares that belong to some 2 x 2 block of wall are houses; the rest are yard walls. Each solid block is
split into rectangles (largest first, growing right then down) and each rectangle becomes one house: walls 2.2
units tall (more for large houses), a gable along the longer side with 0.18 of overhang, thatch for small village
houses and slate for big ones (Vallaki is all slate), a stone chimney on most, and window pieces on every third
open face. Roofs are flat-shaded meshes; their texture comes from world position (`cel_world.gdshader`), the same as
the boxes. A house standing between the camera and the party's leader is cut away: its roof and wall pieces hide
and its walls drop to 0.5 units (`TownBuilder.cut_away`, called each frame with the tree fade).

## Making more art

`tools/art/prop_recipes.json` holds the prompts. A sheet is four objects in a 2 x 2 grid, one Gemini call, cut
apart by `blender/lib/propsheet.py` (each shape goes to the quarter its box centre is in; ruled lines Gemini
sometimes adds between the quarters are removed). Singles are for big things (the cottage, the tent, the collapsed
barn). The cleanup is the walk sheets' (`docs/art/p4_cast_art_pass.md`): bilateral smoothing, area downscale to
about 256 texels per world unit (512 max, never upscaled), palette snap, despeckle and small-clump merging.

    make props GENERATE=1 ONLY="sheet_name"     # new or replaced sheets (2 calls at a time, backoff on errors)
    make props                                  # re-cut every sheet from its source
    make import && python3 tools/art/set_import.py art/sprites/props/*.png && make import

This pass made 43 sheets (157 pieces) and one texture (`wild/water`) in 45 Gemini calls (one sheet was redrawn).

## Checking it

`tools/art/preview/location_tour.tscn` builds a location as in the game, then shoots an overview and close shots
around its doors, props and containers:

    make capture SCENE=res://tools/art/preview/location_tour.tscn LOCATION=death_house_ground NAME=tour ARGS="--lit --shots=6"

`--lit` adds a work light for checking placement in dark interiors; `--hour=` sets the time outdoors.

## Not right yet

- Billboards turn to face the camera, so a long piece (a bar, a pew) drawn on each square of a run reads as a row
  of pieces rather than one long one.
- Wall pieces and door leaves are flat: seen along the wall they disappear, as the cut-away walls' faces do.
- Houses are rectangles; a building the data draws as an L becomes two houses with their own roofs.
- Interiors stay dark at dusk and night by design; the party's lantern lights the pieces near the party.
