# Rule deviations

Every place the game departs from the 2024 rules, with the reason. A rule that simply isn't built yet is "not
started" in coverage.md, not a deviation.

Revisit names what will close a row: an item in the Improvement Ideas note (F4, F10 ...) with its lane, or "—". A
row that waits on a plan phase fails `make validate` once that phase is built (tools/data/check_rules_docs.py).

| Rule | What the game does | Why | Revisit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temporary Hit Points don't stack: "you decide whether to keep the ones you have or gain the new ones" | The engine keeps the larger amount for you, a change of shape included (a creature's own, when they're more, go with it into a Polymorph's shape and come back out; Wild Shape's stay after the shape ends, a spell's vanish with it) | Nothing in the game makes the smaller amount the better one: Temporary Hit Points last until a Long Rest whatever gave them, and what needs them (Armor of Agathys, a Polymorph's shape) lasts while any remain, as the 2024 text says; a prompt would ask the same question every time | — |
| Combining game effects: the most potent of identical effects applies | Potency is approximated by the size of the effects' numbers (conditions weigh most); ties go to the most recent | The rules leave "most potent" to judgment | If two versions of the same spell ever compete in a way the approximation gets wrong |
| Frightened (positional parts) | "Can't willingly move closer" holds only for a source the creature can see, and is read strictly: no square closer to it than where the move began. A source that has died frightens no more (no Disadvantage, nothing to back away from) | A creature can't keep away from what it can't see; the strict reading never lets a frightened creature end closer; the rules leave a dead source to the DM | — |
| Diagonal moves past a wall corner | A diagonal step is refused when either square beside it is a wall or low obstacle | The 2024 grid rules leave corners to the DM; this is the common table ruling and stops moving through wall seams | — |
| Climbing | A rise or drop of 5 ft (a dais, a stair) costs nothing extra; anything higher is climbed, up or down, at 1 extra foot per foot (2 in Difficult Terrain, none extra with a Climb Speed) | The maps draw stairs and daises as 5 ft steps: climbing each one would make every dais cost double | — |
| Going over an edge | A creature forced off a ledge 10 ft or more high, or into a map's open drop, falls, unless it can fly or has a Climb Speed or Spider Climb and isn't Prone or Incapacitated: then it stops at the edge. A push into a ledge 10 ft or more above it stops there | The rules leave catching yourself to the DM; flyers and wall-crawlers (vampires, Strahd) holding on keeps one shove from ending a boss fight | — |
| Falling out of the fight | A creature that goes into a map's open drop (a chasm, the castle's well) falls the map's `drop_ft` and is out of the fight: a foe is gone, a party member rejoins the party when the fight ends. Drops the location doesn't measure are taken as 200 ft (20d6, the most a fall can do) | The map has no bottom to land on | — |
| Feather Fall, Slow Fall | Used when something falls, unless the reactor's rule for it is Never: Slow Fall whenever the fall hurts; Feather Fall by the falling creature or the nearest ally who can see it, for a fall of 20 ft or more or one that could drop it | A fall can't pause for a prompt | When a fall can pause |
| Dragging a grappled creature | It goes into the square its grappler left, or the nearest open square still in the grapple's range (the grappler's reach); with none, the grapple ends. No leaping with Jump while dragging | The rule says the grappler drags it, not where it ends up | — |
| High ground (owner's house rule, 2026-10-07) | A ranged attack (weapon, thrown or spell) from 10 ft or more above its target gets +2 to hit; one from 10 ft or more below gets -2. Height counts the floor and how far a creature is off it; a 5 ft step doesn't count | Not in the 2024 rules: the owner's pick, from Baldur's Gate 3, so that climbing is worth a turn | — |
| Initiative ties | Higher Dexterity first, then the party | The rules leave ties to the DM | — |
| Identical monsters | Share one Initiative roll and act one after another | 2024 DMG option for groups; keeps turns quick | — |
| Spirit Guardians as it's cast | Creatures already in the Emanation when it's cast make the save then (Radiant or Necrotic damage, half on a success), as if it had entered their space | The 2024 text has the save when the Emanation enters a creature's space, when one enters it and when one ends its turn there; whether its appearing counts is left to the DM. Owner's reading (playtest, 2026-10-08): it does | — |
| Divine Smite | The 2014 rule, not the 2024 spell (owner decision 2026-10-08): no Bonus Action and not a spell cast (it doesn't count as the turn's one spell with a slot, can't be countered and breaks no Invisibility), on any number of melee hits a turn while slots last. By default the paladin is asked after each melee hit, Opportunity Attacks included (Critical Hits double its dice); armed on the Spells tab it smites every melee hit that turn without asking; the class tab's rule can make it Automatic (every hit) or Off. 2d8 Radiant plus 1d8 a slot level above 1st, at most 5d8, and 1d8 more against Fiends and Undead. Paladin's Smite's free use still comes first | The owner prefers the 2014 paladin: smiting every attack with the slots to spend | — |
| Study DC | 10 + the monster's CR | The rules leave the DC to the DM | — |
| Weapon juggling | A character can attack with any weapon it carries (drawing it as part of the attack) and the previous one is assumed dropped or stowed | The 2024 free equip/unequip per attack covers most cases; owner decision 2026-10-08: hands aren't tracked | — |
| Material components and focuses | A component without a cost, and a focus, is assumed carried (every pregen has a focus or pouch), and a hand is always free to use it and to make Somatic gestures, whatever the caster holds; so Bladesinger's Weapon Focus has nothing to unlock. A costly component is tracked: the spell's material (Diamonds, Diamond Dust, Incense, a Pearl...) must be carried and is used up when the spell says so, with items of one kind counting together toward the cost (three 100 gp Diamonds for Revivify's 300 gp diamond). A costly focus the spell keeps that isn't a plain gem (Summon Undead's gilded skull, Clairvoyance's jeweled horn, Warding Bond's rings) counts as part of the caster's focus | Owner decision 2026-10-08: costly components only, so a 300 gp diamond is a real errand but nobody is refused a spell over what's in their hands (tracking hands made weapon-and-shield casters put their weapons away, PR #3 review; Bladesong has no free-hand rule either). Counting worth together saves a gem of every value | — |
| Monster Hit Points | Average from the stat block on Balanced (or a fight's own tuned number). Story gives enemies 25% fewer, Tactician and Honour 20% more, never outside what their Hit Dice could roll; Honour puts a boss's lighter version back to its stat block (combat/difficulty.gd) | The 2024 default, with difficulty modes (F1) inside the range a DM rolling Hit Points could get | — |
| Difficulty bonuses (F1) | Story: +2 to the party's attack rolls and saving throws. Tactician and Honour: +2 to enemies' attack rolls and the DCs of their spells and actions. Each shows in its Breakdown under the mode's name | Owner decision 2026-10-07: modes like Baldur's Gate 3's, +2s included; Balanced keeps the 2024 numbers | — |
| Death on Story difficulty | A party member who would die (three failed Death Saving Throws, or massive damage) is left Unconscious and Stable at 0 Hit Points instead | Story mode (F1): nobody in the party dies for good; the other modes use the rules | — |
| Knocking Out a Creature | Chosen ahead by the attacker's Knock Out rule (class tab, Off by default) rather than at the blow, only with weapon attacks and Unarmed Strikes (not melee spell attacks), and only by the party's creatures: foes never knock anyone out | A blow's damage can't stop for a question, and a standing rule is how Baldur's Gate 3 does it; the story has no use for captured heroes | When damage can pause mid-resolution |
| After a won fight | Dying party members are stabilized at 0 Hit Points (their friends tend them) | Rolling death saves every 6 seconds out of combat with nobody hostile adds nothing; the rules let anyone stabilize them with a DC 10 Medicine check | — |
| Forcing a lock | A Strength (Athletics) check against the lock's DC + 2 | The rules leave forcing to the DM; slightly harder than picking keeps Thieves' Tools worth carrying | — |
| Disarming a trap | Failing by 5 or more springs it | A common table ruling; the 2024 rules leave failure to the DM | — |
| Finding and springing traps | Only an explicit check finds a trap (a Search, Find Traps, a Wand of Secrets); passive Perception never does. A found trap still springs when someone steps on it, and paths don't go round it; it can be set off on purpose from within 5 ft (whoever stands in it takes it), and disarming is always the player's choice | The owner's call (2026-10-08): the 2024 rules let passive Perception notice traps; here finding one is a choice the player makes | — |
| Traps in a fight | A location's traps are armed in its fights: any creature, friend or foe, that walks or is shoved onto one springs it, found or not (a flyer passes over); a pit drops its victim the pit's depth and leaves it Prone where it fell, without the climb out; a chandelier that is a trap is a thing in the fight instead (break its chain) | The owner's call (2026-10-08) that traps fire in fights too; a pit fight with its bottom modelled would need depths on the grid | — |
| Merchants' prices (U11) | A friendly merchant charges ×0.9 and pays ×1.1, a hostile one ×1.25 and ×0.75. One Persuasion check (DC 15 unless the shop says) wins ×0.9 and ×1.1 more with that merchant for good; a failure is final with that merchant (the owner's rule for failed social checks) | The 2024 rules leave prices and haggling to the DM; plan §5.6 asks for prices that follow attitude and a Persuasion haggle | — |
| Raise Dead (the ordeal) | A raised hero has -4 on D20 Tests for 10 days, then none (story/services.gd) | Owner's call (2026-10-07): the 2024 spell's penalty shrinks by 1 with each Long Rest instead | — |
| Temple services and inn rooms (F14) | St. Andral's sells Cure Wounds, Lesser Restoration, Remove Curse and Raise Dead at the PHB's spellcasting-service prices, read by Father Lucian from the church's scrolls. A booked room at an inn gives the next Long Rest there temporary Hit Points equal to each hero's level (twice that, and one more level of Exhaustion gone, in the best room) | Lucian's stat block casts none of those spells (owner, 2026-10-07: the church keeps scrolls); the 2024 rules give lodging no effect, and a room needs a reason to buy it when resting at the inn is free | — |
| Long Rest interruption | In a "risky" place, a 1 in 6 chance (never on Story difficulty); an interrupted rest gives a Short Rest's benefits | Travel has random encounters (Phase 4), but a rest doesn't start one | F16's night visitors at camp, lane 24 |
| Sneaking up (who notices whom) | While the party sneaks, each member keeps the Stealth total rolled on starting; a foe notices a member it sees clearly (less than Three-Quarters Cover) when its passive Perception, 5 lower if the member stands in dim light to it, is at least that total. A member out of its sight or behind Three-Quarters Cover goes unnoticed | The 2024 rules only let you hide out of sight and leave noticing to the DM; comparing Stealth with passive Perception is the usual table call for sneaking up, and the light and cover parts follow the rules for obscurement and Hide | — |
| Facing before a fight | A foe waiting in plain view, or a townsperson watching for a theft, sees only within a 120° cone the way it faces (out to 30 ft) and hears the party all round within 10 ft, not through walls; Stealth still applies to both. In fights, nobody has facing | Owner pick 2026-10-07, after Baldur's Gate 3's sight cones: sneaking up behind someone should work. The 2024 rules have no facing, so fights keep them | — |
| How far foes notice | A foe waiting in plain view notices the party only within 30 ft | The rules set no distance; without one, foes would notice the party across a field or the length of a hall, and at 60 ft a camp beside a path saw everyone who walked past (the maps are compact) | U10, lane 25: the reach shows on the ground |
| Surprise and noise | A party that isn't sneaking surprises no foe (it's heard coming), and a fight a foe starts by noticing the party surprises no one | The rules leave who is caught unawares to the DM | — |
| Hiding before a fight | A sneaking member nobody noticed, with a Stealth total of 15 or more and Three-Quarters Cover from every foe, starts the fight with the Invisible condition, as if they had taken the Hide action; it ends when the fight does | The rules don't say whether hiding before a fight carries into it; this asks what the Hide action asks | — |
| Picking a pocket | Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) against the target's passive Perception, once per person; a picked pocket holds coins by the target's station plus any items their data names | The 2024 rules leave the DC and what a pocket holds to the DM | — |
| Who sees a crime | The same notice rule as foes in plain view (30 ft in their sight cone without Three-Quarters Cover, or 10 ft by ear; passive Perception against Stealth while sneaking); a crime nobody sees has no consequence | The rules leave witnesses to the DM; one rule for noticing keeps sneaking predictable | — |
| Trespass | Seen in a private room, the party is told to leave and gets three more steps; seen after that, or after coming back, it's a crime | A DM's warning before the watch is called | — |
| Exploration movement | On the grid, one square at a time, followers stepping into the square ahead. Turn-based exploring (F7) moves one member at a time, up to their Speed in a round of six seconds, with no actions counted | ADR 0009: one geometry for exploring and fighting; the 2024 rules have no rounds outside combat, so the mode only borrows their movement | — |
| Flying | A flyer rises and sinks 5 ft at a time where it stands (1 ft of movement per foot) and keeps its height over the floor as it moves, following the floor up a ledge or down a drop for nothing extra. 5 ft up it passes over creatures, low walls, crates and furniture; chasms and deep water it crosses at any height; walls stop it (they reach the ceiling, or are trees and towers). Indoors the ceiling is 20 ft unless the map says otherwise; outdoors flyers go up to 60 ft. Monsters the AI plays keep to the ground | The maps don't draw ceilings or the tops of walls; heights count in 5 ft steps like the grid | — |
| Ready | A readied attack or spell waits for one of three triggers, picked with a right-click: an enemy the creature can see comes within its reach or range, or one already within it attacks or casts a spell. The Reaction comes once that attack or spell is done | The 2024 trigger is any perceivable circumstance; a grid fight can't take free-form triggers, and these are the ones that matter in one | — |
| D20 Test choices made in the middle of a roll (Indomitable, Stroke of Luck, Mage Slayer's Guarded Mind, Heroic Inspiration on a save, Tactical Mind, a Bardic Inspiration die, Countercharm, Bend Luck, Dark One's Own Luck, Fanatical Focus, Steel Yourself, Symbiote's Vigor, Mist Step, Knowledge from a Past Life, Shared Resilience, Noble Scion, Alustriel's Mooncloak, the save responses of feats and spells) | Asked (or settled by the creature's rule) wherever the roll can pause: spells' saves (Sleep, Command, Polymorph, Banishment and Resilient Sphere included), monsters' save actions, auras and the riders on their hits, Topple, lair actions, the areas a creature starts or ends its turn in, repeated saves, Death Saving Throws, Concentration saves (see below) and attack rolls. Only asked when it could change the result. Elsewhere (saves inside other spells' own code such as Eyebite and the high-level spells' rays, features' and items' saves, areas a creature walks into, ability checks) the roll still can't wait, so each is used on a failure unless its rule is Off, or only on Automatic for the Reactions that were Automatic-only before | Those rolls happen deep inside code that can't stop yet; failing the save is almost always worse | F6, lane 2: the remaining saves |
| Concentration save after damage | A failure that a choice could still turn (Heroic Inspiration, Indomitable, a Bardic Inspiration die...) is asked about once the attack, spell or action that dealt the damage has finished, with the other reactions to it (at the latest as the turn ends); Concentration holds until then. With nothing to ask, it's settled at once as before | The damage is dealt deep inside the attack's or spell's resolution, which can't stop in the middle | — |
| Coming down from the air | A creature off the floor that nothing holds up any more (knocked Prone, held to Speed 0, its flight ending, let go of while carried) comes down at the end of the move, action or turn that grounded it; one that comes down in another creature's space lands in the nearest open square, and a flyer can't sink into a creature below it | Effects that ground a flyer resolve inside one action; the rules don't say what happens when a falling creature lands on another | — |
| Areas and height | An area laid on the ground reaches as high as its size (a sphere's radius, a cube's side, a cylinder's or wall's height); one from the caster reaches from the caster's own height (an emanation its size, a cone half its distance, a line its width). Points are chosen on the ground | The grid has no vertical aiming; this keeps a Fireball on the ground from catching a creature 30 ft up | — |
| Falling into deep water | Halved with a Reaction and a DC 15 Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check (2024), then the creature swims to the nearest open square | Swimming in a fight isn't built | — |
| Heroic Inspiration gained while you already have it: "you can give it to a player character in your group who lacks it" | When a story moment awards it to someone who already has it (story/in_character.gd, F15), it goes to the first party member in marching order who lacks it | A conversation can't stop for a pick; giving it away is never worse than losing it | If story notices ever take a choice |
| Lucky's Advantage | Armed from the hotbar for the next D20 Test; Lucky against an attack is a prompt | The rule lets you decide after seeing the situation, before the roll | — |
| Portent | Assigned from the hotbar to a creature's next D20 Test | Same as the rule, chosen ahead of the roll | — |
| Cast-time choices | The first option is the default; the hotbar's right-click menu picks another | Keeps one click for the common case | — |
| Commander's Strike, Crown of Madness, Maneuvering Attack | The player picks Commander's Strike's ally (within 60 ft, able to see or hear you) and then the creature it attacks, and the ally uses whichever of its attacks does best there; Crown of Madness's caster names the creature to attack (or no one) when casting and with each keep-control Magic action, and that choice holds until the next; Maneuvering Attack's ally is one of the attacker's allies, picked right after the hit. A crowned party member is still played by the AI on its turns, even when no victim is in its reach | The weapon and the moment of the "mental" choice are the closest one-click readings; handing a crowned party member's free turn back to the player needs the turn's controller decided once at its start | — |
| Charger, Lunging Attack | "Moved this turn" stands in for "moved 5 (10) ft in a straight line toward the target" | The grid path isn't tracked as straight lines | — |
| Disarming Attack, Heat Metal: which object | The object dropped is picked for you: the weapon in the target's main hand, else what its other hand holds (a monster: the weapon it holds) | Saves a choice step in the middle of a hit | — |
| A monster's weapons | A monster holds one weapon at a time, the first melee weapon of its stat block (else its first weapon); a Disarming Attack, Heat Metal, Command's "Drop" or falling Unconscious drops that one, and the others stay usable, as if slung or sheathed. Its thrown weapons and ammunition aren't counted. A weapon attack's item is the item its id names, else the gear it matches (a sword cane, which no item matches, can't be carried off by anyone else). The AI takes its weapon back only when it lies within reach; it doesn't walk to it | Stat blocks don't say what's in hand or how many javelins a monster carries | — |
| Things on the ground | A thrown weapon comes down in its target's space, hit or miss (for a big target, the square nearest the thrower); a creature picks up what lies within 5 ft of it, a pile of one thing (two daggers) with one interaction; only a character carries things off (a monster takes back its own weapon); a dead creature keeps what it held; a donned Shield is never dropped. What a creature lets go of lands on the floor of its square, however high it is. A creature going over the edge into a map's open drop keeps hold of what it carries (it goes down with it); a thing that comes down over the drop is out of reach for the rest of the fight, and the party fetches it from below with the rest when the fight ends (a foe's weapon joins the loot) | The rules leave where a thrown weapon lands, how far a creature can reach for an object and what can be fetched back up to the DM; a party member who falls comes back after the fight the same way | — |
| Recovering ammunition | When a fight ends each party member gets back half the mundane ammunition it shot (rounded down) on its own; magic ammunition isn't found again | The minute's search is the minute a won fight already takes; which magic shots hit and lost their magic isn't tracked | — |
| Levitate, Fly | Levitate lifts its target 20 ft (less under a low ceiling) and holds it there; it can't pull itself along walls or a ceiling, and a caster can't raise or lower another creature it levitates.
