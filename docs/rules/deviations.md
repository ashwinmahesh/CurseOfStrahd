# Rule deviations

Every place the game departs from the 2024 rules, with the reason. A rule that simply isn't built yet is "not
started" in coverage.md, not a deviation.

Revisit names what will close a row: an item in the Improvement Ideas note (F4, F10 ...) with its lane, or "—". A
row that waits on a plan phase fails `make validate` once that phase is built (tools/data/check_rules_docs.py).

| Rule | What the game does | Why | Revisit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temporary Hit Points don't stack: "you decide whether to keep the ones you have or gain the new ones" | The engine keeps the larger amount for you, a change of shape included (a creature's own, when they're more, go with it into a Polymorph's shape and come back out; Wild Shape's stay after the shape ends, a spell's vanish with it) | Nothing in the game makes the smaller amount the better one: Temporary Hit Points last until a Long Rest whatever gave them, and what needs them (Armor of Agathys, a Polymorph's shape) lasts while any remain, as the 2024 text says; a prompt would ask the same question every time | — |
| Combining game effects: the most potent of identical effects applies | Potency is approximated by the size of the effects' numbers (conditions weigh most); ties go to the most recent | The rules leave "most potent" to judgment | If two versions of the same spell ever compete in a way the approximation gets wrong |
| Frightened (positional parts) | "Can't willingly move closer" holds only for a source the creature can see, and is read strictly: no square closer to it than where the move began. A source that has died frightens no more (no Disadvantage, nothing to back away from) | A creature can't keep away from what it can't see; the strict reading never lets a frightened creature end closer; the rules leave a dead source to the DM | — |
| Diagonal moves past a wall corner | A diagonal step is refused when either square beside it is a wall or low obstacle | The 2024 grid rules leave corners to the DM; this is the common table ruling and stops moving through wall seams | — |
| Climbing | A rise or drop of 5 ft (a dais, a stair) costs nothing extra; anything higher is climbed, up or down, at 1 extra foot per foot (2 in Difficult Terrain, none extra with a Climb Speed) | The maps draw stairs and daises as 5 ft steps: climbing each one would make every dais cost double | — |
| Going over an edge | A creature forced off a ledge 10 ft or more high (on natural ground, a cliff 15 ft or more high), or into a map's open drop, falls, unless it can fly or has a Climb Speed or Spider Climb and isn't Prone or Incapacitated: then it stops at the edge. A push into a ledge 10 ft or more above it (a cliff on natural ground) stops there; one down a steep slope slides without falling | The rules leave catching yourself to the DM; flyers and wall-crawlers (vampires, Strahd) holding on keeps one shove from ending a boss fight | — |
| Natural slopes (owner, 2026-10-08) | A map's natural ground (its `elevation` rows) changes height in 5 ft steps between squares: 5 ft is a gentle slope (nothing extra), 10 ft a steep one (5 extra feet going up, like Difficult Terrain; nothing extra going down), 15 ft or more a cliff, climbed up or down at 1 extra foot per foot (none extra with a Climb Speed). Out of a fight the party's walks go round cliffs where they can and climb them where they can't, with no check | The 2024 rules leave the lie of the land to the DM; a 5 ft grid can only tell slopes apart by how much they rise per square, and the walk out of a fight never asks for checks | — |
| Jumping down | A creature at the top of a cliff (or a built ledge 10 ft or more high) can jump down to the square below beside it: 5 ft of its movement, then the fall (1d6 per 10 ft, Prone unless unharmed). Medium or smaller creatures only | The rules let anyone drop off a ledge and fall; one square's move and the fall keeps it a single choice in the menu, the way Baldur's Gate 3 offers it | — |
| Long Jump (owner pick, 2026-10-09: Jump with an arc) | A Long Jump slot on the hotbar for everyone: up to the Strength score in feet when the creature has moved 10 ft or more this turn (its run-up), half from standing, in whole squares, each foot costing a foot of movement; it clears creatures and rough ground but not walls, and lands only on a free square. Leaving a foe's reach on the way draws an Opportunity Attack as any movement does. The obstacle-height limit and the DC 10 Acrobatics check for landing in Difficult Terrain aren't rolled; High Jumps aren't built | The run-up needn't be in a straight line or right before the leap, and the landing check is skipped, to keep the leap one click with an arc to show it | — |
| Ground that hides | Rising ground between two creatures blocks sight and gives cover the way a wall does: the lines from the top of the one looking to points down the other (its top, then lower) are traced over the ground, and ground above a line blocks it. A ridge hides, a small rise leaves the head showing (Three-Quarters Cover), and from the top of a hill the far side is in sight | The rules leave terrain cover to the DM; tracing the lines over the grid's own heights decides it the same way every time | — |
| Falling out of the fight | A creature that goes into a map's open drop (a chasm, the castle's well) falls the map's `drop_ft` and is out of the fight: a foe is gone, a party member rejoins the party when the fight ends. Drops the location doesn't measure are taken as 200 ft (20d6, the most a fall can do) | The map has no bottom to land on | — |
| Feather Fall, Slow Fall | Used when something falls, unless the reactor's rule for it is Never: Slow Fall whenever the fall hurts; Feather Fall by the falling creature or the nearest ally who can see it, for a fall of 20 ft or more or one that could drop it | A fall can't pause for a prompt | When a fall can pause |
| Dragging a grappled creature | It goes into the square its grappler left, or the nearest open square still in the grapple's range (the grappler's reach); with none, the grapple ends. No leaping with Jump while dragging | The rule says the grappler drags it, not where it ends up | — |
| High ground (owner's house rule, 2026-10-07) | A ranged attack (weapon, thrown or spell) from 10 ft or more above its target gets +2 to hit; one from 10 ft or more below gets -2. Height counts the floor and how far a creature is off it; a 5 ft step doesn't count | Not in the 2024 rules: the owner's pick, from Baldur's Gate 3, so that climbing is worth a turn | — |
| Initiative ties | Higher Dexterity first, then the party | The rules leave ties to the DM | — |
| Shared party turns (owner, 2026-10-09, after Baldur's Gate 3) | Party members and guests the player runs who stand next to each other in the initiative order take their turns together: the player switches between them freely (a click on a hero's portrait or frame, Tab, the radial's Inspect) and can leave one mid-turn and come back. Each hero's own turn still starts the first time they're taken (or when the one before ends) and ends when it's ended, so everything keyed to a creature's turn happens once, at its own time; a foe, the lair's count 20, a hero the AI plays (Command, fear) or a second turn of the same hero ends the run. Settings, Game, Party turns: One at a time plays the rules' order | The rules give every creature its own turn in order; sharing adjacent turns lets the party plan together, the owner's pick from Baldur's Gate 3 | — |
| A guest who keeps out of fights (owner, 2026-10-18: Ireena "doesn't attack, and enemies don't attack her"; 2026-10-24: Gertruda too) | A story ally whose NPC says `guest_fights: false` is never in the encounter: she takes no turn, can't be targeted or caught in an area spell of either side, and can't fall. Her figure stays on the board as a bystander whose square blocks like any bystander's, and she steps to the nearest free square away from the foes if the fight starts on one someone will stand on. The Narrator says so once and now and then after | Ireena is a CR 1/8 noble with about 9 Hit Points, which made players leave her behind and so lose her story; Gertruda is a commoner with 4 Hit Points taken home to her mother, and a fight would only be where she dies; the field is per NPC and can be turned back on in data | — |
| Flanking (owner, 2026-10-12) | The 2024 DMG's optional Flanking rule is on: a creature has Advantage on melee attack rolls against a foe when it and an ally are both next to that foe on opposite sides or corners of its space (for a bigger creature, on sides across from each other). The ally must be able to act and see the foe, and so must the attacker. Heroes and foes alike; the odds on hover name it. Foes on Tactician and Honour move to flank; on Story and Balanced they fight as before and take the Advantage only when it falls their way. Settings, Game, Flanking: Off plays without it | An optional rule the owner asked for; it rewards moving round a foe. Balanced foes chasing flanking squares made three hard side-quest bosses unwinnable | — |
| Sharing a square (owner, 2026-10-12; foes 2026-10-19) | A creature the player moves may end its move in an ally's space, so heroes can share a square, with no limit (each figure stands a little to one side). Foes the game moves may share too (owner: "Enemies should be able to share the same square"): a foe may end its move in an allied foe's square while the square's load stays within four, a Tiny creature counting 1 and a Small or Medium one 2 (two Medium foes, four Tiny ones, or one Medium and two Tiny); a Large or bigger creature never shares, and no one shares with an enemy. A foe takes a square of its own first: it shares an ally's square only when none of its own lets it strike a target (never just to walk up), so a pack in the open fights as it did and a foe stuck behind its fellows in a corridor or a doorway still reaches its target. A place's `allies_block` still stops all sharing. A move that can't end in a square says who holds it and why (an enemy's space, a full square, a creature too big to share). The figures standing in a shared square are pointed at one by one: the mouse picks the one it is over, and on a controller the cursor and the next-target button walk through them, with a tooltip line naming who else is there; an Invisible foe in the square neither shows nor shifts the others. Out of a fight nobody blocks anybody: the party walks through people and people through the party (foes waiting in plain view still block) | The 2024 rules let you move through an ally's space but not end there (four Tiny creatures fit in a space); rooms full of furniture made fights indoors cramped, and the owner wants foes to be able to crowd as heroes do. Large creatures are left out because their space is several squares and a stack under part of one has no layout | — |
| Small decor (owner, 2026-10-12) | A '=' square holding only small decor (a stool, a basket, sacks, candles or lamps on stands, jars, cushions, books on the floor, a small crate) is open floor, in fights and out of them; furniture, barrels, crate stacks and containers still fill their squares | The rules leave obstacles to the DM; small things on the floor shouldn't wall off a room | — |
| Identical monsters | Share one Initiative roll and act one after another | 2024 DMG option for groups; keeps turns quick | — |
| XP for foes (XP levelling, owner's ask 2026-10-14) | Each foe is worth 40% of its stat-block XP, split by a fixed four whoever travels (guests, a short party and a benched hero change nothing). A foe that withdraws, flees or is taken counts; a creature on the neutral side or a summon doesn't | The 2024 DMG gives the party the foes' full XP split among its members and any NPC who helped. The game's fights were scaled up for 2024 heroes and field far more foes than the book's, so by-the-book XP puts an average player two to three levels above the fights' tuning by the Wizard of Wines; 0.4 keeps them on it (tools/balance/xp_sim.py). A fixed split means nobody sends an ally home for more XP | — |
| Minor milestones in XP levelling | A side or companion quest finished and a place found on the travel map are worth half of the DMG's Low XP budget for a character at the party's level; a new location a tenth, a find, a passed check, a trap noticed or disarmed a twentieth, a lore entry a fortieth. A region milestone is the High budget and a main-quest goal Moderate; a region's climax quest is paid by its milestone, not twice | The DMG says a minor milestone is a Low encounter and a major one High, but the game has hundreds of small finds; the shares keep an average player within a quarter level of the milestone levels (docs/plans/xp_mode.md §3, §5) | — |
| The floor and the ceiling (XP levelling) | A region milestone lifts the party to the level milestone mode would give, and its XP can never reach the level two above that: one-time XP over the ceiling waits for the next milestone, and XP from the road, the camp and Strahd's visits over it is lost | Every fight and boss is tuned for the milestone level; the 2024 rules have no such limit. Without it a bare-minimum player ends four levels behind and a thorough one is at the cap by the Werewolf Den | — |
| Strahd's visits (XP levelling) | A visit he withdraws from pays the High budget, not his stat block's 13,000 XP, which is paid when he is destroyed | He leaves each fight alive, so his CR 15 would otherwise be earned many times over | — |
| Spirit Guardians as it's cast | Creatures already in the Emanation when it's cast make the save then (Radiant or Necrotic damage, half on a success), as if it had entered their space | The 2024 text has the save when the Emanation enters a creature's space, when one enters it and when one ends its turn there; whether its appearing counts is left to the DM. Owner's reading (playtest, 2026-10-08): it does | — |
| Divine Smite | The 2014 rule, not the 2024 spell (owner decision 2026-10-08): no Bonus Action and not a spell cast (it doesn't count as the turn's one spell with a slot, can't be countered and breaks no Invisibility), on any number of melee hits a turn while slots last. By default the paladin is asked after each melee hit, Opportunity Attacks included (Critical Hits double its dice); armed on the Spells tab it smites every melee hit that turn without asking; the class tab's rule can make it Automatic (every hit) or Off. 2d8 Radiant plus 1d8 a slot level above 1st, at most 5d8, and 1d8 more against Fiends and Undead. Paladin's Smite's free use still comes first. It goes off beside a smite spell armed for the same hit (Shining Smite's extra damage is automatic; Divine Smite is still asked, or armed, on that hit, and the answer is for that hit alone) | The owner prefers the 2014 paladin: smiting every attack with the slots to spend | — |
| One spell with a spell slot per turn (owner's house rule, 2026-10-13) | A Reaction spell (Shield, Hellish Rebuke, Feather Fall, Backlash, Moment of Prescience, a War Caster spell instead of an Opportunity Attack) can be cast with a slot whatever the creature cast earlier in the turn, its own turn included, and a slot spent on one doesn't stop a slot spell later in that turn. The limit still holds between the turn's Magic action and its Bonus Action | The 2024 limit counts every slot spent on a turn; the owner rules that Reactions don't count towards it (Ratatouille wasn't asked about Shield against an Opportunity Attack after casting a level 3 spell earlier in his turn) | — |
| Eldritch Blast as a War Caster Reaction (Ashwin's ruling, 2026-10-19) | Cast with the Reaction in place of an Opportunity Attack (War Caster's Reactive Spell), Eldritch Blast sends one beam whatever the Warlock's level; Agonizing Blast, Repelling Blast, Grasp of Hadar and the other invocations still apply to that beam. The prompt names the one beam when it is offered and the log says what it gave up and why. Cast as an action it keeps every beam, and so does one readied and released (the action was spent readying it) | The 2024 Reactive Spell asks for a spell with a casting time of an action that "must target only that creature", and every beam of Eldritch Blast may be aimed at one creature, so by the book a level 17 Warlock fires four beams at a foe leaving reach. The owner rules that a Reaction is one attack like the Opportunity Attack it replaces, so one beam (combat/spell_specials.gd REACTION_BEAMS) | — |
| Study DC | 10 + the monster's CR | The rules leave the DC to the DM | — |
| Weapon sets (2024: a weapon is equipped or unequipped as part of each attack of the Attack action, or with the free object interaction) | A character attacks only with what it has equipped: the weapons in hand and its second weapon set (the inventory's set II). An attack with a weapon of the second set takes that set in hand first, and Swap weapons on the hotbar does the same, both free and as often as it likes (only the player's own attack does: an Opportunity Attack, any other reaction and a foe's move use what is in hand, so the sets never swap on their own, Ashwin 2026-10-26); a Shield goes with its set. A weapon that's only in the pack can't be drawn in a fight. New heroes, and older saves once as they load, get a second Light weapon in a free off hand beside a Light one and the best weapon of the other kind (a bow beside a sword) in an empty second set | Owner, 2026-10-09: "we shouldn't be allowed to use weapons that aren't equipped", with Baldur's Gate 3's two weapon sets and their free switch as the model; replaces the weapon-juggling deviation of 2026-10-08 | — |
| Material components and focuses | A component without a cost, and a focus, is assumed carried (every pregen has a focus or pouch), and a hand is always free to use it and to make Somatic gestures, whatever the caster holds; so Bladesinger's Weapon Focus has nothing to unlock. A costly component the spell uses up is tracked: the spell's material (Diamonds, Diamond Dust, Incense, Holy Water...) must be carried and is used up, with items of one kind counting together toward the cost (three 100 gp Diamonds for Revivify's 300 gp diamond). A costly component the spell keeps isn't needed at all: Identify's pearl, Chromatic Orb's diamond, Programmed Illusion's jade dust, Gate's diamond, Songal's Elemental Suffusion's pearl, and the costly foci (Summon Undead's gilded skull, Clairvoyance's jeweled horn, Warding Bond's rings). What a caster has to hand is still recorded (`Character.focus_item`): an item of their classes' focus kind (a Cleric's or Paladin's holy; the Holy Symbol of Ravenkind is one, Ashwin 2026-10-18) in the focus slot or a hand; nothing asks for it in a fight, and a class with another focus or none doesn't gain it | Owner decisions 2026-10-08 (costly components only, so a 300 gp diamond is a real errand but nobody is refused a spell over what's in their hands: tracking hands made weapon-and-shield casters put their weapons away, PR #3 review; Bladesong has no free-hand rule either) and 2026-10-09 (only the components a spell uses up). Counting worth together saves a gem of every value | — |
| Monster Hit Points | Average from the stat block on Balanced: the 2025 Monster Manual's, or Curse of Strahd's for the adventure's own creatures (Ravenloft: The Horrors Within's for the wereraven). A boss (narrative/combat/bosses.json, or a foe with legendary actions) may still carry a fight's own tuned number until the boss pass sets it to its book value too; a creature no book has (`source.book` custom, such as the Amber Temple's amber golems) keeps the game's own numbers. Story gives enemies 25% fewer, Tactician and Honour 20% more, never outside what their Hit Dice could roll; Honour puts a boss's lighter version back to its stat block (combat/difficulty.gd) | Owner, 2026-10-24: "for ALL monster HP, just use the Monster Manual values; no custom HP", and fights are made harder by hitting harder, not by Hit Points. The difficulty modes (F1) stay inside the range a DM rolling Hit Points could get | The boss pass (boss fights lane), for the bosses' tuned numbers |

| Merchants' prices (U11) | A friendly merchant charges ×0.9 and pays ×1.1, a hostile one ×1.25 and ×0.75.
